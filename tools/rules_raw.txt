FIRST PRINCIPLES
In any game, there are certain conventions for all players to be aware of. In Necromunda, these include such things as how to roll and read dice, how distances are 
measured, when it is acceptable to change one’s mind, and so forth.
FIGHTERS, VEHICLES & MODELS
In games of Necromunda, players use finely detailed models to represent their 
gangs on the tabletop, and to represent the Vehicles of the ash wastes. In game 
terms, it helps to be precise in how such things are defined so that players can 
easily interpret the rules:
Any model that represents a single humanoid or a single animal is referred to as a 
‘Fighter’ in the rules and has the Fighter Type. This could be a lowly gang member, 
a renowned hired gun, or even an exotic beast owned by a mighty gang Champion.
Any model that is Mounted on an animal or bike is a Fighter with the Mounted 
Subtype.
Any model that represents a Vehicle of any type, be it a small Vehicle operated by a
single driver or large Vehicle crewed by many, is referred to as a Vehicle in the rules
and has the Vehicle Type.
Where the rules use the terms Fighter or Vehicle they are referring specifically to 
models of that Type. However, where the rules use the term ‘model’ or ‘models’ 
they are referring to both Fighters and Vehicles.
CONTROLLING & OPPOSING PLAYER
Frequently, rules will refer to the controlling player – this is the player whose model
is being affected; the opposing player is their opponent. 
TAKE-BACKS & CHANGING ONE’S MIND
It is not uncommon for players of any game to second guess themselves 
occasionally, saying they are about to do something before immediately changing 
their mind. As a general rule, players should be tolerant of this in their opponents, 
as they will likely do it themselves! However, once dice have been rolled for any 
reason, players must abide by their decision; they can no longer go back and 
change anything that came before the dice roll.
MEASURING DISTANCES
In games of Necromunda, all distances are measured in inches (“) with a range 
ruler or tape measure. Distances can be measured at any time by either player.
Distances between models and any other battlefield objects (terrain features, 
objectives, and so forth) are measured from the closest point of one base to the 
closest point of the other. If a model or an object does not have a base, measure to
or from the closest point of the model or object overall.
MEASUREMENT AND SOLID TERRAIN FEATURES
Some terrain features, such as the thick walls that separate corridors, the floors 
and ceilings that separate levels, or large rock formations looming up from the 
landscape are defined by the rules as solid. Unless stated, distances cannot be 
measured through any terrain feature defined as being solid. 
If two models are at different elevations, or levels, measure the distance directly 
from one base to the other.
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: No Pre-Measuring
If an Arbitrator wishes, in their campaign they may represent the 
uncertainty of gang warfare in the underhive by allowing players to 
measure distances only after an action has been declared. If the 
action is not possible (due to a target being outside a weapon’s Long 
Range) the model still attempts it, and would still make a shot and so
potentially suffer the effects of the Unstable, Ammo(X+) and similar 
traits. For example, with the Unstable trait a Hit roll would still be 
made to see if the weapon backfires, but regardless of the result the 
shot would miss.
LINE OF SIGHT & COVER
Visibility in Necromunda is vitally important; models need to be able to see 
where their enemies are in order to shoot at them, but in the dense and dark 
terrain of the underhive, or within the thick clouds of dust and pollution that 
hang over the ash wastes, a clear and unobstructed Line of Sight is often hard to 
find.
FIGHTER VISION ARCS
Fighters are assumed to be constantly looking around them, keeping a wary eye, 
ear, and nose out for danger. As such, all models have a 360° vision arc.
THE ARC (X) TRAIT
A weapon with this trait may only target enemy models in the model’s arcs 
denoted by X.
If the arc is Front, Rear, Left or Right, determine the arcs by drawing two 
imaginary lines through the corners of the model. If the arc is Centreline, 
determine the arc by drawing two imaginary lines along the side of the model.
LINE OF SIGHT
A model's Line of Sight (i.e., what they can see) is determined by the presence of 
terrain and other models, and can be checked at any time. 
To check one model's Line of Sight to another model, stoop down to look from 
the first model's point of view.
• If a straight, uninterrupted line can be drawn from the first model to any part of
the other model* the first model has a Line of Sight to them.
• If no such line can be drawn to the other model due to intervening terrain or 
the presence of other models, the first model does not have a Line of Sight to 
them.
*Excluding their base or any insignificant elements, such as a protruding 
hairstyle, the barrel of a gun, or a spike from their armour.
LINE OF SIGHT & SOLID TERRAIN FEATURES
Solid terrain such as the walls between corridors, the floors and ceilings between 
levels, or the large rock formations looming up from the landscape are, by their 
nature, impossible to see through. Unless stated otherwise, a Line of Sight can 
never be drawn through any terrain feature defined as being solid.
AGREEING ON LINE OF SIGHT
It pays to be gracious when your opponent claims Line of Sight. If 
both players take this approach, the game will flow more smoothly. If
players cannot agree whether one model has a Line of Sight to 
another, it should be settled by rolling off.

COVER
In the majority of cases, the Line of Sight between one model and another will 
need to be checked for the purposes of shooting and, in many cases, a model that 
wishes to shoot at an enemy model may be able to draw a Line of Sight to only part
of their intended target.
To see if a target is in cover, stoop down to look from the attacker’s point of view in 
exactly the same manner as described for checking Line of Sight. However, when 
checking to see if the target is in cover, disregard any friendly Fighters in base 
contact with the attacker – moving them aside temporarily if necessary, as long as 
they return to the original place once the shot has been resolved:
• If the target is fully visible, they are not in cover (i.e., they are in the open).
• If any of the target is obscured by terrain or models they are in cover.
As with Line of Sight, ignore the target’s base and any minor elements such as hair, 
gun barrels and spikes – and again, if a decision cannot be reached, the players 
should roll off.
The benefits of cover are discussed in more detail under the rules for making Save 
rolls.
TERRAIN
Terrain plays a vitally important role in Necromunda. Not only does it provide a 
dense and challenging battlefield for gangs to fight over, but it also instills battles 
with the character of the world in which those gangs exist; whether that is the 
desolate ash wastes, the terrifying Underhells of Hive Secundus or the buried toxic 
domes of the underhive. How terrain can impact the game and the rules governing 
specific terrain features will be presented elsewhere within this book (see 
Movement). There are, however, a few features that should be dealt with here as 
general principles.
Impassable Terrain: Any terrain can be designated impassable when setting up the 
battlefield, regardless of size. Impassable terrain may include solid sections of wall, 
vertiginous rock formations or towering vats of molten metal. Impassable terrain 
cannot, as the name suggests, be moved through or over.
Difficult or Dangerous Terrain: Difficult and dangerous terrain ranges from acidic 
pools of sludge to debris strewn floors. Any terrain can be designated as difficult or 
dangerous when setting up the battlefield, regardless of size. Difficult or dangerous 
terrain, as the name suggests, is difficult to move over and can even be deadly to 
those traversing it.
Walls: Walls are almost always considered impassable terrain. On a battlefield of 
only one level, it is assumed that at the top of each wall sits a ceiling preventing 
Fighters from climbing over a wall. However, on a battlefield that covers multiple 
levels, featuring ladders, staircases and even lifts, it is possible for Fighters to 
scale and climb over walls.
Doors: These come in various sizes but their rules are the same regardless of size 
– they are set up as part of the Pre-battle Sequence. By default, all doors are 
closed at the start of a battle, unless a scenario specifies otherwise. Closed doors 
are considered impassable terrain. They cannot be moved through and block 
both Line of Sight and measurement in the same way as walls and solid terrain as 
described previously.
Any Fighter may open a closed door or close an open door within 1” of them by 
sacrificing 2” of their movement during their activation.
Closed doors can be targeted by attacks and are automatically hit. All doors have 
a Toughness of 5. if a door is Wounded four times it is destroyed (do not roll 
Injury dice for doors) and removed from the battlefield.
Obstacles & Structures: Obstacles are any freestanding terrain features 
measuring no more than 2” high and no more than 2” across, such as barricades, 
barrels and pipelines.
Structures are any terrain features measuring more than 2” high or more than 2” 
across, be they freestanding or connected to other terrain features in some way. 
Structures may feature many different levels, platforms and walkways on which 
models may be placed.
Both obstacles and structures may interrupt Line of Sight and offer cover to 
models as described previously and in the Shooting rules.
Fighters may climb over obstacles or up onto structures as described in the 
Movement rules, but players should be aware that movement may be reduced or
not possible in some cases. Vehicles are unable to climb over obstacles or up 
onto structures.
AGREEING ON TERRAIN TYPES
After players have finished setting up the battlefield, it is worth going
through each piece of terrain on the battlefield and agreeing what 
they all count as to avoid confusion during the battle.

NECROMUNDA DICE
Necromunda uses several different types of dice to determine the outcome of 
various actions and events. These are:
D6
The most frequently used dice in Necromunda is a regular six-sided dice, marked 1 
to 6. On many Necromunda dice it is common for the ‘6’ to be replaced by a logo – 
either that of the game or a specific gang.
D66
In some cases, players will be instructed to roll a D66. To do this, roll two D6, one 
after the other, counting the first dice as tens and the second dice as units, to give a
result between 11 and 66. For example, a roll of 2 then 4 would give a result of 24.
D3
The rules might also call for a D3 to be rolled, but an actual three-sided dice is not 
necessary. To roll a D3, simply roll a D6 and modify the result by halving it, round 
fractions up.
FIREPOWER DICE
Unique to Necromunda, a Firepower dice is used when a model makes a ranged 
attack with a weapon that has the Rapid Fire (X) trait. It is rolled to determine how 
many shots a rapid fire weapon can make and so see if it runs Out of Ammo. This is
a six-sided dice, with three bullet symbols on one face, two bullet symbols on two 
faces, one bullet symbol on two faces, and a bullet symbol plus an ammo symbol 
on one face. 
SCATTER DICE
This is a six-sided dice, marked with a Hit symbol (a target icon with a small arrow) 
on two faces and an arrow on each of the other four. The Hit symbol also 
incorporates a small arrow. This dice is used to determine random directions, and is
most often used when firing weapons that have the Blast (3”/5”) trait.
INJURY DICE
Another dice unique to Necromunda, Injury dice are special dice used to determine
the severity of the Injury a model suffers, ranging from Injured/Damaged to being 
taken Out of Action. This is a six-sided dice, with an Injured symbol on two faces, a 
Seriously Injured symbol on three faces, and an Out of Action symbol on one face.
Throughout the rules, players will encounter certain terms that relate to the rolling 
of dice.
ROLLING DICE
A game of Necromunda uses dice rolls a great deal. The roll of a dice determines 
many things, be it a model’s ability to keep their Cool in the heat of battle, or the 
effectiveness or a shit fired from a lasgun. Throughout the rules, players will 
encounter certain terms that relate to the rolling of dice.
SINGLE DICE ROLLS
The rules will often require a player to roll a single dice, in which case the rules will 
state 'roll a D6'. This is a single dice roll. Sometimes, the rules might require several
single dice to be rolled at once. For example, roll 3D6 means three dice are rolled 
together, but the result of each dice is considered separately. These are also single 
dice rolls.
MULTIPLE DICE ROLLS
Often the rules will require a player to roll multiple D6 and add the results 
together. For example, the rules might instruct a player to roll 2D6. In such cases, 
2D6 are rolled and the results added together to give a total between 2 and 12. 
This is a multiple dice roll.
DICE POOLS
Sometimes, the game may require two or more dice to be rolled and a single 
result selected - for example, a player might roll two or three Injury dice and 
select one result to apply. In this case a pool of dice has been rolled. 
TESTS
Often, when making a single dice roll or a multiple dice roll, a player needs to roll 
a specific number or higher. The rules refer to this as a 'test', which is shown as a 
number followed by a '+'. Making a test is simple . If a player is required to roll a 
4+ on a single D6, a roll of 4, 5 or 6 would be a success, but a roll of 1, 2 or 3 
would not.
CHECKS
Sometimes, when making a single dice roll or a multiple dice roll, a player needs 
to roll equal to or under a specific number. The rules refer to this as a 'check,' 
which is shown as a number. Making a check is simple. If a player is required to 
roll equal to or under a 3 on a single D6, a roll of 1, 2 or 3 would be a success, but
a roll of 4, 5 or 6 would not. 
MODIFYING DICE ROLLS
The rules will often call for a dice roll to be modified, particularly when making a 
test. To do this, simply roll the dice and then add or subtract the modifier(s) 
shown, effectively changing the outcome of the dice roll. If the rules ever require 
a player to divide a dice roll in any way, any fractions should be rounded up, 
unless the rules state otherwise. 
If a +1 modifier is applied to a test, add it to the dice result; if it is applied to a 
check, add it to the target number. As a rule of thumb, positive modifiers make it 
more likely for the test or check to pass, negative modifiers less likely. 
If both positive and negative modifiers are applied to a dice roll, apply all positive 
modifiers first, then apply all negative modifiers. 
ROLLING OFF
The rules may call for both players to roll a dice (usually a D6). Occasionally, a 
characteristic of some sort will be added to the result. This is called 'rolling off' 
and the highest score always wins a roll-off. In the case of a tie, roll again unless 
otherwise instructed. 
NATURAL ROLLS
A 'natural' roll is the actual number rolled on a dice, regardless of modifiers 
(including the halving of the result role on a D6 when rolling it as a D3). It is not 
uncommon for a natural roll to grant a bonus or a penalty, or to grant an 
automatic success or cause an automatic failure, regardless of any modifiers that 
might apply to the dice roll. 
RE-ROLLS
When rolling dice, it is common for things to go wrong. However, occasionally 
there are special rules that allow for certain dice rolls to be re-rolled. in which 
case:
• The second result must always be accepted, even if it is worse than the original 
result.
• A dice can never be re-rolled more than once, regardless of the source of the 
re-roll.

MODEL STATUS
A model’s Status dictates what they can or cannot do. The actions a model can perform during their activation will depend heavily upon their current Status, which can
change through the course of a game. This is generally represented by how the model is positioned on the battlefield, and tokens placed either next to them on the 
battlefield or on their Model Card, whether they are Active, Engaged, Suppressed or Seriously Injured/Damaged, as described below, but may also depend upon 
other factors.
ACTIVE
A model is Active if they are not currently Engaged with any enemy models, 
Suppressed or Seriously Injured/Damaged. Active is the default Status for a model
and such models enjoy the greatest freedom to perform actions.
ENGAGED
In order for models to make close combat attacks, they must be Engaged with an 
enemy model:
• If the base of a Fighter is touching the base of an enemy Fighter, they are said to 
be in base to base contact and are Engaged with that enemy Fighter, unless either 
Fighter is Seriously Injured/Damaged.
• If the base of a Fighter is within 1” of any part of an enemy Vehicle at the start of 
their activation, they may choose to be Engaged with that Vehicle, unless either 
model is Seriously Injured/Damaged.
• If any part of a Vehicle is within 1” of any part of an enemy model at the start of 
its activation, it may choose to be Engaged with that model, unless either model is 
Seriously Injured/Damaged.
• At all other times, models are not considered to be Engaged with enemy Vehicles 
and vice versa.
An Engaged model can generally only choose actions that allow them to fight or 
retreat, but other factors may increase the number of available options.
SERIOUSLY INJURED/DAMAGED
If a model should suffer a ‘Serious Injury’ result on an Injury dice, they will become 
Seriously Injured if they are a Fighter, or Seriously Damaged if they are a Vehicle. A
model is very vulnerable whilst this is their Status, being susceptible to enemy 
attacks against which they cannot defend themselves and being unable to move 
quickly towards safety.
If a Seriously Injured/Damaged model regains a Wound, change their Status to 
Active; if this happens while they are within 1” of an enemy model, they are 
instead taken Out of Action.
A model that is Seriously Injured/Damaged has a Seriously Injured marker placed 
next to them.
SUPPRESSED
Models generally become Suppressed as a result of a being hit by enemy fire, and
may perform one less action during their activation. If a Suppressed model ever 
becomes Engaged by an enemy model, they will immediately change their Status 
to Engaged. A model can never be both Suppressed and Engaged.
A model that is Suppressed has a Suppressed marker placed next to them, 
representing the model keeping their head down to avoid enemy fire. This 
marker is removed at the end of their next activation.
MULTIPLE STATUSES
A model may only ever have one Status at a time. When multiple 
Statuses would apply to a model, apply them in the following priority
order:
1. Seriously Injured/Damaged
2. Engaged
3. Suppressed
4. Active
SHOWING CONDITIONS & STATUSES
There are a variety of Conditions and Statuses that can be applied to models 
during play, which can be indicated by placing markers next to models or on their 
Model Cards. The following types of markers can placed next to models to 
indicate their Status or Condition:
• Ready & Activated markers
• Suppressed marker
• Seriously Injured/Damaged markers
• One Wound & Two Wound markers
• Injured marker
• Insanity marker
• Webbed marker
• Out of Ammo marker
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: Laying Fighters Down
Some veteran players may remember earlier editions of 
Necromunda where Fighters were laid down on the battlefield to 
show their Status. This has been changed in this edition due to 
difficult with some models in laying them on their fron or back. 
However, if players wish, they may represent the Suppressed Status 
by laying the model on their back, and the Seriously Injured Status 
by placing the model on their front.

FIGHTER SUBTYPES
All Fighters have a Subtype that dictates their position within a gang and which Post-cycle Actions they may perform. Fighters may also have multiple different 
Subtypes applied to them. For example, a Cawdor Sheen Bird is a Fighter (Beast, Flying, Pet) meaning that they are a Fighter that is subject to the rules of the Beast, 
Flying and Pet Subtypes.
BEAST
A Beast is an animal that can only perform limited actions. Unless stated otherwise,
a Beast may not perform the following actions:
• Interact
• Treat Ally
Additionally, a Beast may not assist a model making a Recovery test and a gang 
cannot use the Leadership characteristic of a Beast for Bottle checks unless every 
friendly model on the battlefield is a Beast.
BRUTE
All Brutes have the Juggernaut skill. When a Brute is removed from the Gang 
Roster, all weapons and wargear they are equipped with are deleted instead of 
going into the gang's Stash.
CHAMPION
Friendly models within 6" and in Line of Sight of a Champion may use the 
Champions Cool characteristic for Nerve checks.
All Champions have the Inspiring skill (see Skills).
FLYING
A Flying Fighter may move between levels without needing to climb a wall or 
ladder by measuring a straight line between their starting and final positions. They 
may move over terrain and other Fighters in this manner, though they still need to 
move up and over the terrain. Additionally, whenever they fall, they are not 
Suppressed and never take any damage. They may not, however, ignore 
impassable terrain and may not end their movement with their base overlapping 
an obstacle or another Fighter's base.
GANGER
If during the Post-cycle Sequence a Ganger should reach at least 37 XP, they are 
promoted to a Champion. After rolling to determine an Advancement, they replace
the Ganger Subtype with the Champion Subtype, keeping all their other existing 
Subtypes. From that point onward they are treated as a Champion of the 
appropriate gang. This does not cause them to change their Fighter entry – if an 
Escher Gang Sister becomes promoted to a Champion she remains an Escher Gang 
Sister, she does not become an Escher Death Maiden or Escher Matriarch.
HANGER-ON
When selecting a gang's starting crew and creating their Reinforcements deck, 
Hangers-on may be excluded from being randomly selected. When a Hanger-on is 
removed from the Gang Roster, all weapons and wargear they are equipped with 
are deleted instead of going into the gang's Stash.
LEADER
One Fighter in every gang must always have the Leader Subtype; if they die, 
another Fighter is promoted to replace them (see Death of a Leader). Friendly 
models within 12" and in Line of Sight of a Leader may use the Leader’s Cool 
characteristic for Nerve checks.
All Leaders have the Inspiring skill (see Skills).
LONER
A gang cannot use the Leadership characteristic of a Loner for Bottle checks unless 
every friendly model on the battlefield is either a Beast or a Loner.
MOUNTED
Many items of wargear grant a Fighter the Mounted Subtype. The following rules 
apply to Mounted Fighters:
Optional Equipment: When determining which models are available for a 
scenario, the controlling player may decide if any Fighter with wargear that grants
the Mounted Subtype will use that wargear during the battle. This decision is 
made for all Fighters before determining starting crews and cannot be changed 
mid-battle. Fighters cannot get off their mounts nor get on another model's 
mount during a battle.
Skills: In addition to their normal skills, all Mounted Fighters gain the Nerves of 
Steel and Hit & Run skills (see Skills).
Mounted Fighters and Terrain: Mounted Fighters cannot climb up nor down walls
or ladders. They can still attempt to jump down to lower levels or jump gaps as 
normal.
Gun It: When a Mounted Fighter performs a Charge or Dash action, they may 
only make one 90° turn during the action.
Agile Subtype: some mounts will grant the Fighter the Agile Subtype as well as 
the Mounted Subtype. Mounted Fighters with the Agile Subtype can make as 
many turns as they wish while performing a Charge or Dash action.
Hands Full: a Mounted Fighter cannot be equipped with any weapon that has the
Heavy or Paired trait unless it also has the Lance or Lance-bomb trait, though 
such weapons may be included on the Fighter’s Model Cards that do not include 
the mount as part of their equipment. Additionally, a Mounted Fighter may not 
use a Secondary weapon in close combat.
PET
Pets are purchased as wargear for a model and as such their credits value is 
included on the owning models Model Card. Pets are automatically included in a 
gang's starting crew if their owner is, and do not count against the crew or 
Reinforcement limits. When their owner is deployed to the battlefield, including 
as part of a Special Rule, any Pets they own are deployed following the rules for 
the model’s deployment within their Leash range of their owner.
• When a model that owns any Pets activates, check if each of their Pets are 
outside of their Leash range; any that are will become Panicked. The model and 
their Pets then activate in an order of the controlling player’s choice, each model 
fully resolving their activation before the next one activates.
• When a model goes Out of Action or is removed from the battlefield for any 
reason, any Pets they have on the battlefield are also removed.
• When a Pet is taken Out of Action, they do not trigger a Bottle check nor do 
they force nearby models to make a Nerve check, though they do have to take 
Nerve checks if other models are taken Out of Action near them.
• Pets cannot control objectives, though they do prevent enemy Fighters from 
controlling them.
PANICKED
When a Pet that is Panicked activates, depending on their Status they must
perform the action dictated by the table below. They perform this action 
even if another rule or condition would cause them to perform a different 
action [for example, the Insanity or Frenzy Conditions).

owner. If they are unable to do so, they must end their activation as close

VEHICLE SUBTYPES
All Vehicles have a Subtype based on their method of locomotion that affects how they interact with both difficult and dangerous terrain. Vehicles may also have 
multiple different Subtypes applied to them. For example, a Van Saar 'Arachni-Rig' Servo-Suit has the Loner and Walker Subtypes.
HYBRID
A Vehicle with this Subtype uses a mix of methods for locomotion; it may choose 
which of its Locomotion Subtypes to use when activating.
Loner
A gang cannot use the Leadership characteristics of a Loner for Bottle checks unless
every friendly model on the battlefield is either a Beast or a Loner.
MANOEUVERABLE
A Vehicle with this Subtype may perform one additional 90° turn when performing 
the Charge or Dash action.
SKIMMER
A Vehicle with the Skimmer Locomotion Subtype ignores all difficult or dangerous 
terrain when moving. However, if it ends its activation on difficult or dangerous 
terrain, a skimmer must make an Agility Test. If the test is passed, it has 
successfully negotiated the terrain. If it is failed, It suffers a Str -, AP-, L1 Hit that 
automatically Wounds and ignores all saves.
TRACKED
A Vehicle with the tracked Locomotion Subtype is designed to keep going over all 
terrain – though they struggle to reach high speeds. A tracked Vehicle suffers no 
penalty for moving over difficult Terrain.
TRANSPORT (X)
A Vehicle with the transport (X) Subtype has an enclosed cab on the Vehicle that is 
designed to carry Fighters. All transports must have at least one clearly visible 
access point.
The number of Fighters able to fit in a Transport is determined by the value of X. 
This is the maximum number of Fighters that can Embark upon the Vehicle. When 
deploying at the start of a battle, Fighters can be deployed embarked upon a 
Transport as long as the Vehicle is already deployed upon the battlefield. Fighters 
cannot start embarked upon a Vehicle that is in Reinforcements (note: Brutes and 
Mounted Fighters cannot enter a Transport).
While embarked on a transport, Fighters are still given Ready markers but in all 
other ways are treated as if they are not on the battlefield, so cannot be the 
target of any attacks or use any abilities. If any Fighters are embarked on a Vehicle
when it is taken Out of Action, they must be placed Suppressed within 1" of the 
Vehicle before resolving any explosion (if applicable). Any Fighter that cannot be 
placed in this way is automatically taken Out of Action – immediately make a roll 
on the Lasting Injury table for them.
If a Fighter performs the Interact action while within 1" of the access point of a 
friendly Transport, remove them from the battlefield. They are now embarked 
upon the Transport.
While embarked upon a Transport, the only action a Fighter may perform is the 
Interact action. If a Fighter performs the Interact action while embarked upon a 
Transport, place them on the battlefield within 1" of an access point and at least 
1" away from any enemy models. This may mean that Fighters cannot disembark 
from a Transport, in which case they cannot perform the Interact action that 
activation.
WALKER
Vehicles with the Walker Locomotion Subtype are highly manoeuvrable and Agile,
but lack the speed of other Vehicles. A Walker may make as many turns during its
activation as it wishes. For every 1" of difficult terrain a Walker moves through, it 
counts as having moved 2".
Additionally, Walkers are treated as if they were Fighters for the purpose of 
seeing if a model is Engaged (see Model Status), this means that enemy Fighters 
in base contact with a Walker are considered to be Engaged with it, may not treat
it as terrain and must obey the 1" rule with it.
WHEELED
Vehicles with the Wheeled Locomotion Subtype are able to reach high speeds but
struggle to maintain their speed over rough terrain. For every 1" of difficult 
terrain a Wheeled Vehicle moves through, it counts as having moved 2".

CONDITIONS
A model’s Status is always either Active, Engaged, Suppressed or Serious Injured/Damaged, but they may also be subject to one or more of the following conditions. 
Conditions are denoted by placing a condition marker either next to the model on the battlefield or on their Model Card as a reminder that the condition is in effect.
The following list of conditions is not exhaustive. Some Conditions are common and will apply to a model many times during a battle. Others are less common and may
be the result of an attack from a weapon with a certain trait; the full rules for such conditions can be found in the corresponding weapon trait rules. Other conditions, 
in particular Out of Ammo, may apply specifically to a weapon the model carries rather than the model themselves. A model may be subject to several conditions at 
the same time.
FEARSOME
If a model subject to the Fearsome Condition is selected as the target of close 
combat attacks, the attacking model must immediately make a Willpower check. If 
the check is passed there is no effect; however, if the check is failed, then until the 
current model’s activation ends, set the attacking model’s WS to 6+.
Models that are subject to the Fearsome Condition are themselves not affected by 
this Condition, unless the enemy model also has the Terrifying Condition.
FRENZY
An Active model subject to the Frenzy Condition must declare a Charge action if 
they begin their activation within their Movement characteristic +6" of an enemy 
model and they are able to complete that Charge. This Charge action must move 
directly towards the nearest enemy model. While subject to the Frenzy Condition, 
a model increases their Attacks characteristic by 1.
HATRED (X)
When a model subject to the Hatred (X) Condition either performs or is the target 
of a Charge or Engage action against a model they hate, they may re-roll any failed 
Hit rolls in close combat for the duration of the current models activation.
Which enemies are hated varies from model to model and will be shown in 
brackets after the name of this Condition (shown here as 'X').
INJURED/DAMAGED
When a model is reduced to zero Wounds they become subject to the Injured 
Condition if they are a Fighter, or the Damaged Condition if they are a Vehicle. An 
Injured/Damaged model gains no benefit from any of their skills; in effect they act 
as if they do not have any skills. If an Injured/Damaged model regains a Wound, 
then they lose the Injured/Damaged Condition.
INTOXICATED
While a model is subject to the Intoxicated Condition, they reduce their BS and 
WS characteristics by 1 to a minimum of 6+, but increase their Cool characteristic 
by 1 (to a maximum of 10).
TERRIFYING
Models that are subject to the Terrifying Condition gain all the benefits of the 
Fearsome Condition. In addition, if an enemy model wishes to perform a Charge 
or Engage action targeting a model that is subject to the Terrifying Condition, 
they must make a Willpower check. If this check is passed then they may perform
the action as normal; however, if the check is failed then they must remain where
they are and their activation ends.
Models that are subject to the Terrifying Condition are themselves not affected 
by either the Fearsome or Terrifying Conditions.
WEBBED
While a model is subject to the Webbed Condition they are unable to move 
(either voluntarily or involuntarily) and suffer a -1 penalty on all Hit rolls. At the 
end of a Webbed model’s activation they make a Strength check. If the check is 
passed they break free and lose the Webbed Condition.
INSANITY
When a model subject to the Insanity Condition activates, roll a D6 on the 
Insanity table to see what action they perform. The model’s controlling player 
decides how the model will perform the action following the guidelines laid out

In Necromunda, each player controls a gang, which is made up of a number of models. Each of these models is referred to as a Fighter or a Vehicle within the rules. 
Each model has a Type (either Fighter or Vehicle) and one or more Subtypes such as Beast, Flying, Leader, Specialist, etc.

Each model has a characteristics profile, which details their capabilities and battle. For example, here is the profile for an Escher Gang Sister:

MODEL CHARACTERISTICS & TESTS/CHECKS
A model’s characteristics are defined as follows:
MOVEMENT (M)
This is the distance, in inches, the model can move when making a standard Move 
action.
WEAPON SKILL (WS)
This shows the model’s proficiency when using weapons in close combat.
BALLISTIC SKILL (BS)
This characteristic represent the model’s proficiency with ranged weapons.
STRENGTH (S)
This is how strong the model is. The higher a model’s Strength characteristic, the 
more likely they are to inflict damage on an opponent in close combat.
TOUGHNESS (T)
This represents how tough the model is. The higher a model’s Toughness 
characteristic, the less likely they are to be wounded by an attack.
WOUNDS (W)
A model’s Wounds characteristic is a measure of how much punishment they can 
take before succumbing to their injuries or damage.
INITIATIVE (I)
A model’s Initiative characteristic is a measure of how quickly they act in combat.
ATTACKS (A)
This is a measure of a model’s ferocity in melee. When a model is Engaged in close 
combat, their Attacks characteristic determines how many dice are rolled, with 
each dice representing one attack made against their enemies.
SAVE (Sv)
This is a measure of both how well armoured and protected a model is and how 
well the model can roll with hits and use cover to minimise the damage they take.
LEADERSHIP (Ld)
A model’s Leadership characteristic represents their ability to issue or follow 
commands in the heat of battle.
COOL (Cl)
A model’s Cool characteristic represents their capacity for keeping calm under 
fire.
WILLPOWER (Wil)
Willpower is a measure of a model’s mental fortitude and resilience against the 
horrors of Necromunda.
INTELLIGENCE (Int)
This represents a model’s mental acuity and ability to apply knowledge.
CHARACTERISTIC TESTS/CHECKS
Players will often be called on to make a characteristic test or check for a model – 
for example, a Ballistic Skill test is made when a model attacks with a ranged 
weapon. Characteristic tests and checks are made as follows:
Weapon Skill and Ballistic Skill tests are made by achieving a target number roll 
on a single D6. If the result is equal to or higher than the characteristic, the test is
passed.
Leadership, Cool, Willpower and Intelligence checks are made by rolling equal to 
or under the characteristic on 2D6. If the result is equal to or lower than the 
characteristic, the check is passed.

=== PAGE 10 ===
To check against any other characteristic presented as a simple number, such as 
Strength or Toughness, roll a D6. If the result is equal to or lower than the 
characteristic, the check is passed.
IMPROVING/REDUCING CHARACTERISTICS
Sometimes, the rules will improve or reduce a characteristic, usually as a result of 
an Advancement or a Lasting Injury. When the characteristic is a simple number, it 
is just increased or decreased by the appropriate amount – for example, if a 
model with Strength 3 improves their Strength by +1 their Strength is now 4.
If the characteristic is given as a target number (for example, a characteristic of 
4+ means a dice roll of 4 or higher would be a success), then it is improved by 
lowering the number and reduced by increasing it. For example, if a model with 
Ballistic skill 4+ improves their Ballistic Skill by +1, their Ballistic Skill is now 3.

here is the profile of a boltgun:

Boltgun 12” 24” 4 -1 2 Ammo (3+), Rapid Fire (1) 55 2
A weapon’s characteristics are defined as follows:
SHORT RANGE (SR) & LONG RANGE (LR)
Each weapon has two ranges: Short Range (SR) and Long Range (LR), usually 
presented as a number of inches. Weapons cannot Hit models outside of their Long
Range. There may be other effects associated with an attack being within either the
Long or Short Range of a weapon; these will be explained when they come up. 
Some weapon ranges are shown as a letter or a '-' rather than a number of inches. 
If a weapon’s range is listed as an 'E', it can only be used in close combat against 
targets that are Engaged with the wielder. If a weapons range is 'T', it uses the 
flame template (see Weapon Traits). Weapons with a range of E or T normally only 
have a Short Range given, not a Long Range. If a range is denoted by a '-', it means 
the weapon cannot be used at that range.
STRENGTH (S)
This is the weapon’s Strength, which is used when making Wound rolls against the 
target. For most weapons, particularly ranged weapons, this is a simple number. 
For close combat weapons, this may be shown as 'S', meaning the Strength of the 
wielder should be used, or an S with a modifier (i.e., S+3), meaning the Strength of 
the wielder is used but with a modifier applied. If a weapon’s Strength is '-' then its 
traits will describe how to resolve hits from it.
ARMOUR PIERCING
This shows how good the weapon is at punching through a target's armour. This 
is a modifier which is applied to the dice rolled to make an Armour save. Most 
often, this will be a negative modifier, but in some cases it may be a positive 
modifier or even a '-'  which denotes no modifier.
LETHALITY (L)
This represents how lethal the weapon is. This in turn indicates how many Injury 
dice are rolled against a target that has been reduced to zero Wounds as a result 
of the attack (see Resolve Hits). If a weapon’s Lethality is '-' then its traits will 
describe how to resolve hits from it. 
TRAITS
Most weapons have one or more traits, each of which gives the weapon a unique
bonus or changes the way in which it works. 
CREDIT COST (CREDS)
This indicates how many credits it costs to purchase the weapon from the Trading
Post.
TRADE POINTS (TP)
This indicates how easily available a weapon is from the Trading Post and how 
many Trade Points it costs.

THE PRE-BATTLE SEQUENCE
Prior to any battle, there are several important steps that need to take place, as listed below. Some of these only apply during campaign play, and can therefore be 
skipped during Skirmish play, as mentioned in the appropriate entries. Whatever the case, this sequence must be followed in the order shown, and must be done 
while both players are present.
The Pre-battle Sequence has seven steps:
1. Make a Challenge & Stake Territory
2. Determine Scenario
3. Set Up the Battlefield
4. Choose Crews
5. Announce Battlefield Effects
6. Gang Tactics
7. Deployment
1. MAKE A CHALLENGE & STAKE TERRITORY
For a battle to be fought, a challenge must be issued and accepted. In campaign 
play, challenges are handled as described by the Arbitrator; the campaigns will 
provide guidance. In Skirmish play, a challenge is issued and accepted when two 
players decide to play a battle.
In Campaign play, an eligible Territory is nominated as the Stake for the battle (as 
described in each campaign).
2. DETERMINE SCENARIO
In Campaign play, the scenario will be generated following the rules for doing so 
(see Scenario Generation Tables). In Skirmish play, players may instead simply 
choose the result on each table to create the scenario they wish to play.
DETERMINING THE ATTACKER AND DEFENDER
In Campaign play, the player who issued the challenge is the attacker. In Skirmish 
play, both players should roll a D6, with the player who rolls the highest choosing 
who is the attacker and who is the defender.
3. SET UP THE BATTLEFIELD
The players now set up the battlefield (see Battlefield Set-Up).
4. CHOOSE CREWS
After the battlefield has been set up, before gangs can be deployed, players must 
select their starting Crews for the battle ahead. The scenario’s Crew table will detail
the method of selection to be used, and how many and what kind of models can be
selected (see Battlefield Set-Up).
RECRUIT HIRED GUNS
Players can spend credits to recruit Hired Guns. In Campaign play, the gang may 
spend credits from those it has in its Stash. In Skirmish play, any unspent credits 
left over from creating the gang can be spent to recruit Hired Guns. Additionally, 
in Campaign play, gangs may be able to recruit Hired Guns through other means, 
such as Boons granted by Territories.
If both players wish to recruit Hired Guns, the player with the higher Gang Rating 
must do so first.
5. ANNOUNCE BATTLEFIELD EFFECTS
In Campaign play, many of the Territories apply a Battlefield Effect to any battles 
fought for their control. Players should announce which Territories are applying 
Battlefield Effects to the upcoming battle and what they are.
In Skirmish play, this step of the Pre-battle Sequence can be ignored.
6. GANG TACTICS
Each player may select one of the Gang Tactics they have access to. During the 
battle, each selected Gang Tactic may be used once.
UNDERDOG BONUS
If one player’s Gang Rating is at least 200 credits less than their opponent’s Gang 
Rating, they may select an additional Gang Tactic they have access to for the 
battle for each full 200 credits difference in Gang Rating. 
7. DEPLOYMENT
The final step of the Pre-battle Sequence is deployment, in which the players, 
starting with the defender, set their gangs up on the battlefield, ready for the 
battle ahead. Each scenario's deployment table will give details of the size and 
location of 'deployment zones,' the name given to the areas in which gangs can 
be set up (see Battlefield Set-Up).

GAME STRUCTURE
ROUND SEQUENCE
Each round is split into three phases, each consisting of a number of steps resolved one at a time.
These are as follows:
PRIORITY PHASE
During the Priority phase:
1. Ready Gangs: Each player gives each of their models a Ready marker.
2. Roll for Priority: Each player rolls for Priority.
ACTION PHASE
During the Action phase:
Activate Gangs: Starting with the player who has Priority, players take turns choosing one of their Ready models to activate.
END PHASE
During the End phase:
1. Bottle Checks: If a player had one or more models taken Out of Action this round, they will have to make a Bottle check for their gang.
2. Check For Victory: Players check who controls each objective and if either gang has won the scenario and so ends the battle.
3. Reinforcements: If either Gang has Reinforcements, they arrive on the battlefield.
THE PRIORITY PHASE
The Priority phase is split into the following steps:
1. Ready Gangs: Each player gives each of their models a Ready marker.
2. Roll for Priority: Each player rolls for Priority.
1. READY GANGS
Each player gives every one of their models on the battlefield a Ready marker.
2. ROLL FOR PRIORITY
Each player rolls a D6; whoever rolls the highest has Priority for the round. If both players roll the same, then whoever did not have Priority last round has Priority. If 
the first roll for Priority is a draw then players re-roll until one player wins.
THE ACTION PHASE
The Action phase consists of just one step:
1. Activate Gangs: Starting with the player who has Priority, players take turns to select one of their Ready models to activate.
ACTIVATE GANGS
When it is a player's turn, they choose one of their Ready models and activate them. When a model activates, they may perform two actions.
If one player has run out of models to activate, the other player can activate all of their remaining models in an order of their choosing. Once all models have 
activated, even if they performed no action during their activation, the action phase ends.
TYPES OF ACTION
The following section covers the types of actions models can perform when activated.
A model cannot perform two actions with the same name in the same activation, even if they are different action types, unless a rule specifically says they can (for 
example, the Rain of Blows skill).
Players should take note that whilst the actions a model can perform are based upon their current Status, performing an action can and will, in many cases, change 
their Status.

=== PAGE 13 ===
There are three types of action a model may perform when activated:
SINGLE ACTIONS
Each single action counts as one of their actions. If an activated model’s first action 
is a single action, it may fully resolve it before declaring its second action, though 
no action can be performed until the previous action has been completed. 
DOUBLE ACTIONS
Performing a double action counts as making two actions. For example, if an Active
model makes a Charge action, it will have used both of its actions and cannot 
perform another during this activation. If a model can only perform one action 
during its activation for any reason, it may not perform a double action. 
FREE ACTIONS
Performing a Free action does not count as using one of your actions.
CHANGING ACTION TYPE
Some effects may change an actions type; for example, when a 
model performs the Charge action, after they have moved they must
perform a Fight action as a Free action.
ACTIONS
The following section covers the actions models can perform when activated. Note that some model Types or Subtypes (notably the Vehicle Type and the Beast 
Subtype) may alter which actions the model can perform.
Unless stated otherwise (for example, due to a weapon trait or Special Rule), a model may not perform the same action twice in the same activation.
MODEL ACTIONS
Models that are not Engaged or Seriously Injured/Damaged can perform the following actions. If they are Suppressed when they are activated, they lose their 
Suppressed marker and may only perform one action instead of the usual two.
AIMED SHOT (DOUBLE)
The model makes an attack with a ranged weapon without the Heavy trait and 
applies a -1 modifier to that Hit roll (see Shooting). 
BRACED SHOT (DOUBLE)
The model makes an attack with a ranged weapon with the Heavy trait (see 
Shooting).
CHARGE (DOUBLE)
The model moves their Movement characteristic plus D6 towards a targeted enemy
model and may move within 1" of enemy models as long as they end the move 
Engaged with the targeted model. If they end this move Engaged with the targeted 
model, they must perform a Fight or Coup de Grâce action as a Free action. 
Otherwise they must move as close as possible to the target.
When a Vehicle performs a Charge action, it may only make one turn of up to 90° 
at any point during the move. If a Vehicle cannot perform sufficient turns to be able
to move directly towards a model, it may not target the model with a Charge 
action. 
COUP DE GRACE (SINGLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. Choose one Seriously Injured 
enemy Fighter within 1" of the Fighter; each player rolls a D6 and adds their 
Fighter’s Strength characteristic to the result. If the activating Fighter’s total is 
equal to or higher than the enemy Fighter’s total then the enemy Fighter 
immediately goes Out of Action. 
CRAWL THROUGH DUCTWAY (DOUBLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. If the Fighter is within 1" of a 
ductway, they may be placed within 1" of the other end of the ductway, provided 
they can be set up so that they are not within 1" of an enemy model and so that 
their base does not overlap that of another friendly model or an obstacle. 
DASH (DOUBLE)
The model may move a distance equal to their movement characteristic plus their 
Initiative characteristic in inches. When a Vehicle performs a Dash action, it may 
only make one turn of up to 90° at any point during the move. 
ENGAGE (SINGLE)
The model moves D6" towards a targeted enemy model and may move within 1" 
of enemy models as long as they end the move Engaged with the targeted model.
If they end this move Engaged with the targeted model, they must perform a 
Fight or Coup de Grâce action as a Free action; otherwise they must move as 
close as possible to the target. 
FIRE ALL (DOUBLE)
This action may only be performed by Vehicles. The Vehicle fires all ranged 
weapons it is equipped with regardless of if they have the Heavy trait or not. Each
weapon may target a different enemy model; declare all Targets before rolling 
any dice.
GROUP ACTIVATION (SINGLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. The Fighter selects any number of
friendly ready Fighters within 3" and makes a Leadership check with a -1 modifier
per Fighter selected after the first. If the check is passed, then after the Fighter 
has completed their activation, each of the other Fighters may be activated in 
turn. Fully resolve each Fighters activation before moving on to the next Fighter. 
This action cannot be performed by a Fighter that has been selected as the target
of this action this round. 
INTERACT (SINGLE)
The model interacts with a part of the battlefield or an object. The rules for the 
object will say what happens when this action is performed. 
MOVE (SINGLE)
The model may move a distance equal to their Movement characteristic.
RELOAD (SINGLE)
Pick one of the models weapons that is Out of Ammo and reload it (see 
Shooting).
REPAIR (SINGLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. Choose one Seriously Damaged 
friendly Vehicle within 1" of the Fighter. The selected friendly Vehicle 
immediately makes a Recovery test.

SABOTAGE (SINGLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. Choose one Seriously Damaged 
enemy Vehicle within 1" of the Fighter and make an Intelligence check for them. If 
the Intelligence check is passed then the enemy Vehicle immediately goes Out of 
Action; if it is failed then the Fighter fails to destroy any critical parts of the Vehicle,
in which case the action fails and is wasted.
SHOOT (SINGLE)
The model makes an attack with a ranged weapon without the Heavy trait.
TREAT ALLY (SINGLE)
This action may only be performed by Fighters. Choose one Seriously Injured 
friendly Fighter within 1" of the Fighter. The selected friendly Fighter immediately 
makes a Recovery test.
ENGAGED MODELS
Models that are Engaged with an enemy model can only perform the following 
actions:
ENGAGED STATUS
If a model is in base to base contact with an enemy model, they are 
usually considered to be Engaged. This limits the actions that are 
available for them to perform.
An Engaged model can never be Suppressed, and if a Suppressed 
model becomes Engaged, they lose their Suppressed marker.
Note that a Seriously Injured/Damaged model can never be 
Engaged, and does not engage enemy models – models may freely 
move within 1" of enemy Seriously Injured/Damaged models.
FIGHT (DOUBLE)
Resolve a combat this model is Engaged with.
RETREAT (DOUBLE)
The model may move a distance equal to their Initiative characteristic. During this 
move they may freely move within 1" of enemy models, but they must end this 
move at least 1" away from enemy models.
SERIOUSLY INJURED FIGHTERS
Fighters that are Seriously Injured can only perform the following actions:
CRAWL (DOUBLE)
The Fighter may move a distance equal to their Strength characteristic in inches. 
DESPERATE ESCAPE (DOUBLE)
The Fighter flees the battle and they go Out of Action. Roll a D6; on a result of 1 or 
2 they roll on the Lasting Injury table, otherwise they are considered to be Out Cold
(see Lasting Injury table).
TEND WOUNDS (DOUBLE)
When the Fighter makes a Recovery test, they roll an additional Injury dice.
SERIOUSLY DAMAGED VEHICLES
Vehicles that are Seriously Damaged can perform the following actions:
CONDUCT REPAIRS (DOUBLE)
When the Vehicle rolls to Recover, it rolls an additional Injury dice. 
DESPERATE ESCAPE (DOUBLE)
The Vehicle flees the battle and goes Out of Action. Roll a D6; on a result of a 1 or
2 it rolls on the Lasting Damage table, otherwise it is considered to have 
Superficial Damage (see Lasting Damage table).
FREEWHEEL (DOUBLE)
The Vehicle may move D6".
SERIOUSLY INJURED/DAMAGED STATUS
When a model is Wounded by an attack, there is a chance they may 
become Seriously Injured/Damaged. A Seriously Injured/Damaged 
model is temporarily unable to continue fighting, though with time 
they may manage to recover and rejoin the fight. Until such a time, 
however, they are vulnerable to attacks from enemy models and 
cannot defend themselves.
A Seriously Injured/Damaged model can only perform the actions 
listed here. If a model starts their activation Seriously 
Injured/Damaged, after they have finished resolving their action, 
they try to get back into the fight by making a Recovery test.
RECOVERY TESTS
After a model that started their activation Seriously Injured/Damaged completes 
their activation, the controlling player makes a recovery test for them by Rolling 
an Injury dice:
• If the result is Out of Action, the model is removed from play and rolls on the 
Lastting Injury or Lasting Damage table.
• If the result is Seriously Injured, the model remains Seriously 
Injured/Damaged.
• If the result is Injured, the model recovers enough to continue fighting or 
restarts their engine. Remove the Seriously Injured/Damaged marker next to 
them.
Note that if a model becomes Seriously Injured/Damaged during their activation,
they do not make a Recovery test at the end of their activation.
Assistance: When making a Recovery test for a Seriously Injured Fighter, one 
friendly Fighter that is within 1" and is neither Engaged nor Seriously Injured can 
offer assistance. If they do so, roll 1 extra Injury dice (note: Vehicles cannot be 
assisted In Recovery tests and cannot assist others).
Multiple Injury Dice: It may happen that due to assistance, items of wargear, 
Gang Tactics or skills, several Injury dice are rolled when making a recovery test. 
Regardless of how many are rolled, 1 Injury dice is picked by the recovering 
model’s controlling player and resolved – the others are discarded, as described 
previously.
Enemy Models: If a model is within 1" of an enemy model that is not Seriously 
Injured/Damaged when they make a Recovery test, then they are automatically 
taken Out of Action – there is no need to roll the Injury dice.

MOVEMENT
During the Action phase, many actions allow models to perform a move. This section deals with how to resolve movement.
MOVING MODELS
When a model moves, the action they are performing will say how far they can move; unless stated otherwise, they do not have to move this full distance. A Fighter 
need not move in a straight line – they can zigzag around terrain as appropriate (note: a Fighter performing a Charge or Engage action should take the shortest route 
possible). A Vehicle, however, must move in a straight line directly forwards, though, and unless stated otherwise, may perform as many turns as it wishes. When a 
Vehicle turns, pivot it around its Central Point. 
THE 1" RULE
Unless they are performing the Charge, Engaged, Retreat or Crawl actions, a model
may not move within 1" of an enemy model unless the enemy model is Seriously 
Injured/Damaged.
When performing the Charge or Engage action, if a model moves within 1" of an 
enemy model, they must end their move in base contact with an enemy model 
(note: this must be the model targeted by the action).
When performing the Retreat action, a model may move within 1" of any enemy 
models, but must end the move at least 1" away from all enemy models. 
When performing the Crawl action, a Fighter may move within 1" and end the 
move within 1" of any enemy models; however, if they are within 1" of an enemy 
Fighter who is not Seriously Injured when they make their Recovery test they are 
automatically taken Out of Action.
OTHER MODELS
Models may not move through other models, either friend or foe.
DIRECTLY TOWARDS & DIRECTLY AWAY FROM
Sometimes the rules will say that a model must move directly towards or directly 
away from another model. To do this, trace an imaginary straight line that crosses
through the centre of each model – the moving model then moves the required 
distance towards or away from the other along this line.
As always, this cannot make a model move through a solid terrain feature or 
other model. Should a model come into contact with a solid terrain feature, or 
other model, it stops and does not move any further.
If this would cause a model to fall off a platform or a ledge, resolve it as described
in the upcoming section (see Falling).
TERRAIN
The battlefields of Necromunda are covered in a wide variety of obstacles and features, many of which affect how models move. The following sections cover the 
various effects such terrain has on the movement of models.
AGILITY TESTS
Many terrain features require models to make Agility tests. In order 
to do so a model needs to roll a D6 – if they roll a 4 or more they 
pass the test. Some abilities and skills may modify this roll; for 
example, all Escher Fighters add +1 to agility tests. Regardless of 
modifiers, a natural 1 is always a fail and a natural 6 is always a 
success.
DIFFICULT TERRAIN
From pools of stagnant sludge or areas of Fallen Rebel deep in the underhive, to 
areas of uneven and rocky ground in the ash wastes – there are numerous things 
that can make terrain difficult to cross. 
When measuring how far a Fighter may move through difficult terrain, treat every 
1" moved as 2".
How a Vehicle interacts with difficult terrain depends upon the Vehicle's Subtype 
(see Fighter Subtypes).
DANGEROUS TERRAIN
Vats of toxic chems, jagged rocks so sharp they can tear through plates of armour 
and drive shafts with ease, and more besides – any terrain features designated as 
dangerous when setting up the battlefield can pose a huge risk to models crossing 
it.
A model treats dangerous terrain as difficult terrain for measuring how far they can
move. Additionally, after moving through dangerous terrain a model must roll a D6;
on a result of a 1, they immediately suffer a Str -, AP -, L1 Hit that Wounds 
automatically and ignores all saves.
OBSTACLES
Obstacles are any free-standing terrain features measuring no more than 2" high 
and no more than 2" across, such as barricades, barrels and pipelines. Fighters 
may cross obstacles as they move, but doing so reduces their Movement by a 
number of inches equal to the height of the obstacle. A Fighter may not end their
movement on top of an obstacle.
STRUCTURES
Structures are any terrain features measuring more than 2" high and/or more 
than 2" across, be they free-standing or connected to other terrain features in 
some way. Fighters may climb up and on to structures and between the various 
levels and platforms of a structure as they move, and may end their movement 
on any level of a structure if there is sufficient space for their base (see Climbing).
IMPASSABLE TERRAIN & SOLID TERRAIN FEATURES
Any terrain can be designated impassable when setting up the battlefield, 
regardless of size. Impassable terrain may include solid sections of walls, lookout 
towers or towering vats of molten metal. Models may not move across 
impassable terrain.
Ductways: Ductways can be represented either by a marker placed on or next to 
a wall, or by a scenic ductway opening modeled onto the wall itself (note: such a 
feature must be present on both sides of the wall).
A ductway can be up to a maximum of 2" in length and can be placed across any 
2" wide wall or any other terrain features that would otherwise be impassable. 
Their presence creates an exception to the rule that Fighters may not move 
across impassable terrain and allows Fighters to crawl through a narrow duct and 
traverse the terrain feature by using the Crawl Through Ductway action.

DOORS
When Fighters are moving, they may open or closed doors they are within 1" of at 
any point during their move. Doing so costs them 2" of movement each time they 
do so. Note that a Fighter can open a door, move through it and then close it 
providing they have sufficient movement to do so.
Locked Doors: some doors have access terminals located near them, allowing them
to be locked against future access. Any Fighter within 1" of a terminal may perform 
the Interact action to either lock or unlock the door. While a door is locked it 
cannot be opened by sacrificing 2" of movement, though it can still be destroyed.
FIGHTER MOVEMENT ON VEHICLES
In Necromunda battles, gang members engage in rooftop fights, 
leaping from one Vehicle to another as they race along.
To represent this, Fighters treat Vehicles like terrain for the purposes 
of movement, climbing up the sides and leaping on to them from 
gantries.
However, trying to balance on the roof of a speeding Vehicle is a 
tricky proposition. Whenever a Vehicle moves, any Fighters on it, 
except those on designated transport areas, must immediately pass 
an Agility test or fall. If they fall, place them 1" away from the Vehicle
in a direction determined by the Scatter dice after the Vehicle has 
finished moving. If this causes them to land within ½" of the edge of 
a platform, they must make another Agility test to avoid falling. If it 
would cause them to hit impassable terrain, move them the shortest
distance so as to be able to be placed. They only need to test once 
during each Vehicle's activation.
Note that objects will automatically fall off a Vehicle if not placed in a
designated transport area, unless they are in base contact with a 
Fighter which passes their Agility Test.
CLIMBING
Fighters can climb up or down any vertical surface to reach a higher level or 
platform of a structure during their movement. For every 1" a Fighter moves 
vertically by climbing, they count as having moved 2".
A Fighter cannot end their activation mid-climb; they must have sufficient 
movement to reach a flat surface. 
Stepping Up & Stepping Down: Whilst moving, a Fighter may freely step up or 
down onto another level or platform of structure, provided that it is no more than 
½" higher or lower than the level they are currently on. If the difference in height is
more than ½", they must climb or jump down. 
Overhangs: When climbing, a Fighter can traverse an overhang as long as it 
protrudes no more than 1" from the vertical surface. Overhangs that protrude 
more than 1" are considered impassable to a climbing Fighter. 
Ladders & Stairs: When climbing a ladder or stairs between the levels of a 
structure, there are no modifiers to a Fighter's movement. 
LEAPING GAPS
A Fighter can cross any gap of 2" or less with no need to roll as long as they have 
sufficient movement to reach the other side and there is space for their base 
there. If wishing to leap across a gap larger than 2", they must make an Agility 
test. If the test is passed, they successfully cross the gap. If the test is failed, they 
fall from the point they were attempting to leap from as described below. The 
only Vehicles that can attempt to cross gaps are Walkers. Walkers leap over gaps 
in the same manner as Fighters, except they only have to make Agility tests if 
attempting to cross a gap larger than their base. 
FALLING HAZARDS
If a model is forcibly moved off a ledge, they must make an Agility test. If the test 
is passed, they remain where they are and are not moved. If the test is failed, 
they fall as described below. 
Railings: If a ledge has railings, a Fighter applies a +1 modifier to the Agility Test 
to avoid falling. A Vehicle gains no benefit to its Agility tests due to railings.
Jumping Down: A model may wish to deliberately jump down to a lower level. 
When a model does so they automatically fall; there is no need to test. Make an 
Agility test for the model to see how well they land; if they pass the test then 
reduce the effect on the following table by one level (i.e., a fall of between 3" and
6" becomes a fall of less than 3", and a fall of less than 3" will not count as a fall, 
and therefore not cause the Fighter to become Suppressed or end their 
activation). If the test is failed, apply the result from the following table as 
normal.

SHOOTING
When a model makes a ranged attack against one or more enemy models, this sequence is followed:
1. Declare the Shot
2. Measure Range
3. Make the Hit Roll
4. Resolve Hits
1. DECLARE THE SHOT
Choose a ranged weapon profile the attacking model is equipped with and 
nominated an enemy model in Line of Sight to be the target. 
2. MEASURE RANGE
Next, measure the distance between the model making the ranged attack and the 
target, to ensure that the target is within range of the weapon. If the target is out 
of range then the attack automatically misses. 
3. MAKE THE HIT ROLL
A Hit roll is a Ballistic Skill test made against the BS of the model making the shot. 
There are many circumstances that may have an effect upon the Hit roll:
• Target is Engaged: If the target is Engaged, then if the shot hits, all Hits must be 
randomly allocated between all models involved in the combat. 
• Rolls of a Natural 1: If the Hit roll is a natural 1, then it misses, regardless of 
modifiers. 
• Rolls of a Natural 6: If the Hit roll is a natural 6, then it hits, regardless of 
modifiers. 
HIT MODELS BECOME SUPPRESSED
When a model is Hit by a ranged attack they become Suppressed, unless they are 
also Engaged or are Seriously Injured/Damaged.
Falling from Heights: If a model becomes Suppressed or Seriously 
Injured/Damaged as the result of being Hit by a ranged attack, while they are 
within ½" of a ledge they must immediately check to see if they fall. If they are 
within ½" of two ledges then they fall off the closer ledge. If they are the same 
distance away the controlling player may choose which ledge to fall off. 
4. RESOLVE HITS
If the BS test is passed when making a Hit roll, a Hit is scored. Each Hit scored is 
resolved (see Resolve Hits).
RUNNING OUT OF AMMO
Although ammunition is plentiful for the most common weapons, some weapons 
use ammo up at such a high rate, or ammo is so hard to come by, that models can 
run out of ammo. This is represented by weapon traits such as Rapid Fire (X) and 
Ammo (X+).
When a weapon is Out of Ammo, it means that weapon cannot be used again until 
a Reload action is taken. Upon successfully completing a Reload action, the 
weapon can be used once more. 
MULTIPLE WEAPON PROFILES
Many weapons have multiple profiles representing different kinds of ammo for that
weapon. When a weapon goes Out of Ammo only the profile used for the attack 
goes Out of Ammo; other profiles may still be used as normal. When the Reload 
action is performed, only one of a weapon’s profiles (of the controlling player's 
choice) has the Out of Ammo Condition removed. 
RAPID FIRE (X)
Some weapons fire a huge quantity of shots at one time; these are represented 
by the Rapid Fire (X) trait. When firing a weapon with this trait, you may also roll 
a number of Firepower dice up to X. If the attack hits, resolve a number of hits 
equal to the number of bullet holes across all the Firepower dice. 
Regardless of whether the shots Hit or not, if any ammo symbols are rolled the 
weapon has run out of ammo. If multiple ammo symbols are rolled then the 
weapon jams and cannot be used again this battle. A weapon with the Ammo 
(X+) trait may be considered to have rolled one additional ammo symbol. 
Weapons with both the Rapid Fire (X) and Light traits can only fire single shots 
when used in closecombat – the Firepower dice is never rolled when using them 
in this manner. 
BLAST & TEMPLATE WEAPONS
The full rules for how both the Blast (3"/5") and Template traits work can be 
found in the Weapon Traits section. Some of the more common situations are 
covered below:
Hitting Engaged Models: If a weapon with either the Template or Blast (3"/5") 
trait hits a model that is Engaged, then every model (friend and foe) that model is
Engaged with is automatically Hit.
Hitting Models on Different Levels: When resolving a Hit from a weapon with 
either the Template or Blast (3"/5") trait, only models on the same level as the 
targeted model may be Hit by the attack. This means if a Blast marker scatters off 
a ledge, it remains on the same level and explodes in midair. 
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: Stray Shots 
If the attack misses, roll a D6 for each model that is within 1" of the 
line along which the range between the attacker and the target was 
measured, starting with the model closest to the attacker. On a roll 
of 1, 2, or 3, that model is Hit by the attack. On a 4, 5, or 6 the shot 
misses them – move on to the next model at risk of being Hit.
If the attack would have caused more than one Hit, follow this 
sequence for every Hit.
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: Target Priority 
If the Arbitrator wishes they can represent the chaos and urgency of 
battle with the following rule: if the closest model in Line of Sight is 
within 12" of the attacking model and is not either Engaged or 
Seriously Injured/Damaged, they must be the target of any ranged 
attack unless the attacking model passes a Cool check.
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: Walking Fire 
If a Rapid Fire weapon scores more than one Hit, the Hits can be split
between multiple targets. The first must be allocated to the original 
target, but the remainder can be allocated to other enemy models 
within 3" of the first who are also within range and Line of Sight. 
Allocate all of the Hits before making any Wound rules.

CLOSE COMBAT
When a combat is resolved due to a model performing a Fight action, each model gets to make their attacks in an order according to their Initiative using the following 
steps:
1. Determine Who Can Fight
2. Choose Weapons
3. Determine Attack Dice
4. Declare Targets
5. Determine Initiative
6. Resolve Attacks
7. Consolidate
1. DETERMINE WHO CAN FIGHT
When resolving a combat, only the activating model and any enemy models Engaged with them may fight. 
2. CHOOSE WEAPONS
Each model selects which weapon(s) they will be using for the combat:
The activating model selects a weapon with either the Melee or Light trait to be their Primary weapon and, if they have two close combat weapons or weapons with 
the Light trait (in any combination), they may select another such weapon to be their Secondary weapon. 
All other models select one close combat weapon or weapon with the Light trait to be their Primary weapon.
Hand Weapons: if a Fighter does not have any weapons with either the Melee or Light traits, they are still considered to have a weapon about their person that can be
used in a fight. This weapon has the following profile: 
SR LR Str AP L Traits
Hand weapon E - S - 1 Melee
Note that if a Vehicle does not have any close combat weapons then it cannot perform any close combat attacks.
3. DETERMINE ATTACK DICE
The activating model calculates their attack dice in the following manner:
Primary Weapon: The number of attack dice with the model's Primary weapon is equal to the model's Attacks characteristic, +1 if they performed the Charge action 
this activation. 
Secondary Weapon: The model only has one attack dice with their Secondary weapon (note: some skills and traits may affect this).
All other models only make one attack with their Primary weapon regardless of their Attacks characteristic (note: some skills and traits may affect this).
Pistols at Close Quarters: Regardless of whether it is selected as a Primary or Secondary weapon, a weapon with the Light trait can only ever make one attack 
regardless of the models Attacks characteristic, skills, traits or any other modifiers; any additional attacks are wasted. 
4. DECLARE TARGETS
When a model makes a close combat attack, it must be against an eligible target. An eligible target is an enemy model they are Engaged with (a model that is in base 
contact). If there is more than one eligible target, close combat attacks can be split between them as the controlling player wishes. 
5. DETERMINE INITIATIVE
Each combatant fights in an order determined by their Initiative characteristic with the following modifiers: 
• If either the model's Primary or Secondary weapon has the Unwieldy trait, the model’s Initiative characteristic is set to 1 before any other modifiers are applied.
• If the model has successfully performed the Charge action this activation, they then increase their Initiative characteristic by 2.
Each model resolves their attacks in an order based on their revised Initiative characteristic, unless they were taken Out of Action or Seriously Injured/Damaged by a 
model acting with a higher modified Initiative. 
Simultaneous Attacks: If multiple models are resolving attacks with the same modified Initiative characteristic, they will attack at the same time. To simplify this, the 
activating models attacks are resolved first, followed by any other models. Causing an enemy model to become Seriously Injured/Damaged or be taken Out of Action 
does not prevent them resolving their attacks if they have the same modified Initiative characteristic.

6. RESOLVE ATTACKS
Starting with the model with the highest modified Initiative characteristic, complete the following steps before moving on to the next highest and so on until all 
models have fought: 
A. Make Hit Roll(s)
B. Resolve Hits
A. MAKE HIT ROLL(S)
Each attack dice is used to make a Hit roll. A Hit roll is a Weapon Skill test made against the WS of the attacking model. Roll separately for each different weapon being 
used by the model and/or for different targets. 
Note that BS is not used to make Hit rolls in close combat, even when a ranged weapon with the Light trait is used. When making a Hit roll for a weapon with the Light 
trait, a WS test is made, exactly as described above. 
Engaging Models Out of Line of Sight: if a model performed either the Charge or Engage action this activation, targeting a model that was not in their Line of Sight 
when the action was declared, apply a -1 modifier to their Hit rolls.
Assists: when a model makes a close combat attack, they can claim an Assist if at least one other friendly model is:
• Engaged with the target of the attack. 
• Not Engaged with any other models from the same gang as the target of the attack.
If a model claims an Assist in this way, they add a +1 modifier to the Hit roll (multiple Assists cannot be claimed).
Interference: When a model makes a close combat attack, they may suffer Interference if at least one other enemy model is:
• Engaged with the attacker.
• Not Engaged with any other models from the same gang as the attacker.
If a model suffers Interference in this way, they suffer a -1 modifier to the Hit roll (multiple models cannot provide Interference to the same attack).
Rolls of a Natural 1: If, when making a close combat attack, the Hit roll is a natural 1, the attack automatically misses, regardless of any modifiers that may apply.
Rolls of a Natural 6: If, when making a close comment attack, the Hit roll is a natural 6, the attack automatically hits, regardless of any modifiers that may apply.
B. RESOLVE HITS
Each attack that scores a Hit is resolved (see Resolve Hits).
7. CONSOLIDATE
If all enemy models that the activating model was Engaged with have gone Out of Action or are Seriously Injured/Damaged, the model may move up to their Initiative
in inches in any direction. During this move, they may move within 1" of enemy models, but must end the move at least 1" away from all enemy models.

2. MAKE A SAVE ROLL
If a Hit results in a successful Wound roll, the model may be able to make a Save roll. In order to make a Save roll, roll a D6 and apply the weapon’s armour-piercing 
characteristic as a modifier. If the result is equal to or greater than the model’s Save characteristic, the Hit does no damage. 
Only one Save roll may be made for each Hit that successfully Wounds, regardless of how many different types of Save a model may have. For example, if a model has 
a 5+ Armour save and a 6+ Invulnerable save, they may only attempt a Save roll against a successful Wound roll with one of these saves.
Players should note that some weapon traits will disallow Save rolls. For example, the Gas trait states that no Save roll can be made. In such cases, regardless of any 
modifiers, no Save roll can be made. 
If a model has been successfully Wounded by several attacks at the same time, then the Hit models opposing player chooses the order in which the attacks are 
resolved. 
Cover: Being behind cover offers a model additional protection against ranged attacks. If a model is in cover then apply the following modifiers to their Save roll:
 
• Target is within the attacking weapons Short Range +1.
• Target is within the attacking weapons Long Range +2.
When a model is Hit by a weapon with the Blast (3"/5") trait that has scattered, check if the model is in cover from the direction of the centre of the Blast marker. If 
the shot did not scatter, check from the direction of the firing model for the targeted model, and the centre of the Blast marker for all other models Hit.
Invulnerable Saves: Some Saves are denoted as being Invulnerable saves. These are not affected by modifiers from any source (for example, an attack's AP and Cover).
Rolls of a Natural 1 or 2: A roll of a natural 1 or 2 when making a Save roll is always an automatic failure, regardless of any modifiers that may apply.
3. INFLICT DAMAGE
If a successful Wound roll is not saved or mitigated, then the target loses a Wound. If this results in the model's Wounds characteristic dropping to zero (or if the 
Wounds characteristic is already zero), the target’s opposing player rolls a number of Injury dice equal to the weapons Lethality characteristic. They then select one of 
the Injury dice and apply the results as follows:
• Injured: The model has a lucky escape and avoids any further damage, remaining Injured or Damaged. An Injured/Damaged model gains no benefits from any of 
their skills.
• Serious Injury: If the target is a Fighter they become Seriously Injured. If the target is a Vehicle it becomes Seriously Damaged. A Seriously Injured/Damaged model 
may only perform limited actions during their activation. If the model is already Seriously Injured/Damaged this result has no effect.
• Out of Action: The model either succumbs to their injuries or suffers enough damage that their Vehicle is rendered inoperable. Either way, they are immediately 
removed from play and will take no further part in this battle. Roll on the last thing Injury or Lasting Damage table for them.
Note that a model's Wounds characteristic may never drop below zero. If they would lose a Wound when their characteristic is already zero, their Wounds 
characteristic remains unchanged.
NERVE CHECKS
Seeing fellow models being taken out nearby can unnerve models. If a model is within 3" of a friendly model that goes Out of Action, they must make a Cool check. If 
the check is failed, they become Suppressed. 
MODELS ON ZERO WOUNDS
If an effect ever results in a model's Wounds characteristic becoming
zero, the model is automatically Injured/Damaged. If an 
Injured/Damaged model has their Wounds characteristic restored to
a positive value they are no longer Injured/Damaged. If a Seriously 
Injured model ever has its Wounds characteristic restored to a 
positive value, they become Active unless they are within 1" of an 
enemy model, in which case they are immediately taken Out of 
Action.
LASTING INJURIES & LASTING DAMAGE
Whenever a model goes Out of Action, the opposing player makes a D66 roll and looks up the result on the relevant table; for Fighters, the Lasting Injury table, for 
Vehicles the Lasting Damage table. During campaign play, the result is applied against the model and a note of the Lasting Injury is made on their Gang Roster. If a 
Lasting Injury/Damage is rolled that a model has already received, then its effects are applied again.
If a Lasting Injury/Damage cannot be applied to a model, either because it grants a skill that they already have or would take a characteristic above or below its 
maximum or minimum, disregard the part that cannot be applied, but apply the rest of the result.

THE END PHASE
The End phase is split into the following steps:
1. Bottle Checks: If a player had one or more models taken Out of Action this round, they will have to make a Bottle check for their gang.
2. Check For Victory: Players check who controls each objective and if either gang has won the scenario.
3. Reinforcements: If either gang has Reinforcements, they arrive on the battlefield.
1. BOTTLE CHECKS
Starting with the player with Priority, if a gang has had at least one model taken Out of Action this round, they must make a Leadership check using their gang's 
highest Leadership characteristic on the battlefield. If this check is failed, one model of the controlling player’s choice flees the battlefield – remove them from the 
battlefield. If the check result is a natural 12 then two models must be selected instead. When choosing which model flees the battlefield, models that are Engaged 
can only be selected if every friendly model is Engaged. Any Seriously Injured Fighters or Seriously Damaged Vehicles removed in this manner must check to see if 
they succumb to their Injuries or Damage (see The Post-Battle Sequence).
Hold Your Nerve: A gang only needs to check if they Bottle during the End phase of a round if they had at least one model taken Out of Action. If they had a model 
taken Out of Action in a previous round, but not this round, there is no need to check. 
VOLUNTARILY FLEEING THE BATTLEFIELD
In the tense gang combat of Necromunda it is often wise for a gang to quit a fight while they are still ahead. This is especially true of ambushes, and when smaller 
crews find themselves taking on larger and more dangerous ones! Once at least a third (rounded up) of a gang's starting crew are taken Out of Action or have fled, a 
gang's controlling player can choose to flee the battlefield at the start of the Check For Victory step. All of the gang's models are removed from the battlefield and they 
automatically lose the scenario. Any Seriously Injured Fighters or Seriously Damaged Vehicles removed in this manner must check to see if they  succumb to their 
injuries or damage (see The Post-Battle Sequence).
2. CHECK FOR VICTORY
At the start of this step, both players determine who controls each objective on the battlefield:
Controlling Objectives: Many scenarios include objectives on the battlefield; these can range from vox broadcast junctions, to factoria control panels or anything else 
that is considered of value to the denizens of the underhive. In order for a gang to control an objective, they must have at least one friendly Fighter without the Pet 
Subtype within 3" of the centre of the objective, and no enemy models (including Pets) within 3" of the centre of the objective. Disregard Seriously Injured/Damaged 
models when determining who controls an objective. 
For the purposes of gaining XP, only one Fighter (of the controlling player's choice) is considered to be controlling each objective. 
Neither Pets nor Vehicles can control objectives, though they do prevent enemy Fighters from controlling them. 
After control of objectives has been determined, check to see if the Ending the Battle conditions for the scenario being played have been met. If they have, the battle 
ends; otherwise the battle continues. 
3. REINFORCEMENTS
If either gang has Reinforcements arriving on the battlefield this round, players take turns deploying them on the battlefield (see Reinforcements).
WYRD POWERS
Wyrds are a distinct Type of Fighter who are able to manifest strange and powerful abilities, bringing death, destruction and worse to the gang wars of Necromunda. 
During a battle, Wyrds are activated just like any other model, with the exception that they may attempt to manifest or disrupt Wyrd Powers.
Manifesting a Wyrd Power may be either a Single, Double or Free action, depending upon the complexity of the Wyrd power the Wyrd is attempting to manifest. This 
will always be shown in brackets after the name of the Wyrd power itself. When a Wyrd is activated, they may choose to attempt to manifest one or more Wyrd 
powers that they know.
Regardless of the Wyrd power(s) that a Wyrd knows, all Wyrds are able to perform the following actions:
• Maintain Control (Single): In order to maintain a continuous effect (as follows), the Wyrd must perform this action. If they do not, the Continuous effect expires at 
the end of their activation. In order to perform this action, the Wyrd immediately makes a Willpower check with a +3 modifier – if the check is passed then the power 
remains in effect until the end of their next activation; if failed, the Power ends immediately.
• Maintain Control (Free): If they wish, the Wyrd may instead choose to perform this action as a Free action at the start of their activation – if they do so then they do 
not add any modifiers to the Willpower check and may not attempt to manifest another Wyrd power this activation regardless of whether or not the Willpower check 
is successful.
• Concentrate (Single): This Fighter applies a +1 modifier to their Willpower characteristic when manifesting any Wyrd powers during this activation.

MANIFESTING WYRD POWERS
In order to manifest a Wyrd power, a Wyrd must make a Willpower check. If the check is passed then the power is successfully manifested.
DISRUPTING WYRD POWERS
Once a Wyrd has declared they are going to attempt to manifest a Wyrd power, before the Willpower check is made, any opposing Active or Suppressed Wyrds that 
are within 18", have Line of Sight of the manifesting Wyrd and are not Injured may attempt to disrupt the power (note: only one Wyrd may attempt to disrupt each 
Wyrd power).
In order to do so, the disrupting Wyrd makes a Willpower check. If the check is successful then the manifesting Wyrd applies a -2 modifier to their Willpower 
characteristic when attempting to manifest the power.
Rolls of a Natural 2: If the Willpower check to disrupt a Wyrd power is a natural 2, the Wyrd power automatically fails to manifest; there is no need to make a 
Willpower check. The disrupting Wyrd then suffers the effects of Perils of the Warp.
Rolls of a Natural 12: If the Willpower check to disrupt a Wyrd power is a natural 12, then the attempt to disrupt automatically fails, regardless of modifiers. The 
disrupting Wyrd then suffers the effects of Perils of the Warp.
PERILS OF THE WARP
Trying to control warp energies is always a risky business. When either manifesting or disrupting a Wyrd power, if the Willpower check is either a natural 2 or a natural 
12 then the Wyrd has lost control of the energies and must roll on The Perils of the Warp table below after the Wyrd power has been fully resolved.
Rolls of a Natural 2: If the Willpower check to manifest a Wyrd power is a natural 2, then the power will always be manifested, regardless of modifiers. Fully resolve 
the effects of the Wyrd power before rolling on the Perils of the Warp table.
Rolls of a Natural 12: If the Willpower check to manifest a Wyrd power is a natural 12, then the power will never be manifested, regardless of modifiers. Immediately 
roll on the Perils of the Warp table

THE POST-BATTLE SEQUENCE
Following any battle, there are several important steps that need to take place, as detailed below. This sequence must be followed in the order shown, and must be 
done while both players are present.
The Post-battle Sequence has five steps:
1. Wrap-up
2. Reassign Territory
3. Receive Rewards
4. Clean House
5. Report Results
1. WRAP UP
During the Wrap-up players determine whether Seriously Injured/Damaged Fighters succumb to their injuries,  if Seriously Damaged Vehicles have irreparable 
damage, and if any models captured by the enemy manage to escape.
Players should note that any other events that take place at the end of the battle will also take place during the Wrap-up.
SUCCUMBING TO INJURIES/IRREPARABLY DAMAGED
If a model is Seriously Injured/Damaged when the battle ends, or if a model fled from the battlefield while Seriously Injured/Damaged, roll a D6 for them during the 
Wrap-up. On a 3 or more, they survive without any further complications, but on a 1 or 2 they succumb to the damage suffered during the battle and are treated as 
having gone Out of Action – a Lasting Injury/Damage roll is made for them.
BEING CAPTURED
After the battle, any models that suffered a Captured result (61-62) on the Lasting Injury/Damage table then roll on the Escape table to see if they manage to get away.
D6 Result
1 Executed: The model dies or is destroyed attempting to escape and is deleted from the Gang Roster.
2-4 Ransomed: The controlling gang must pay the gang which captured the model D6x10 credits during the Update Roster step of the Post-
cycle Sequence or the model dies or is destroyed and is deleted from the Gang Roster, putting all of their equipment into the owning gang’s 
Stash. If the ransom is paid, the model goes into Recovery.
5-6 Daring Escape: The model manages to escape their captors and goes into Recovery.
2. REASSIGN TERRITORY
The player that won the battle takes control of the Territory Staked on the battle. In
the case of a draw while fighting for control of an uncontrolled Territory, the 
Territory is not claimed by either player. In the case of a draw when a controlled 
Territory is at Stake, the Territory does not change hands.
3. RECEIVE REWARDS
Each campaign will detail the rewards the gangs can receive, based on their 
performance during the battle. Any equipment gained is added to the gang’s Stash 
– it can be distributed to models in the Post-cycle Sequence.
GAINING & LOSING REPUTATION
Most campaigns contain a number of ways in which a gang can gain or lose 
Reputation. If a gang both gains and loses Reputation, any gains are applied before 
any losses (note: a gang’s Reputation may never drop below 1).
GAINING EXPERIENCE
During any battle, a model can gain Experience (XP) in a number of ways. The 
standard ways in which XP is gained are detailed here.
A model gains XP for any of the following actions:
• 1 XP if they took part in a battle.
• 1 XP if their action directly causes an enemy model to become Seriously 
Injured/Damaged.*
• 2 XP if their action directly causes an enemy model to go Out of Action.*
   – If the enemy model is a Leader, Champion, Brute or Vehicle, they gain an extra 1
XP.
• 1 XP if they provide Assistance to another Fighter making a Recovery test who 
successfully recovers (note: Vehicles cannot provide or benefit from Assistance).
• 1 XP if they control an objective during the End phase of a round (note: each 
Fighter can only gain XP this way once per battle and Vehicles and Pets cannot 
control objectives).
*Note that models can only gain XP for Seriously Injuring or taking an enemy 
model Out of Action once per enemy model per activation. For instance, if a 
model causes an enemy Fighter to become Seriously Injured and also go Out of 
Action, they would only gain the 2 XP for causing the enemy Fighter to go Out of 
Action.
4. CLEAN HOUSE
During this step of the Post-battle Sequence gangs can dispose of destroyed 
Vehicles or Injured Fighters. Dead Fighters and Destroyed Vehicles are simply 
deleted from the Gang Roster. If their gang had at least one model on the 
battlefield at the end of the battle, all of a dead Fighter’s equipment is added to 
the gang’s Stash. Otherwise, the deleted model’s equipment is lost.
Also at this point, the controlling player can choose to retire models – usually 
because Lasting Injuries/Damage have rendered them unfit for battle. These 
models are deleted from the Gang Roster, and their equipment may be added to 
the gang’s Stash depending on their Type/Subtype.
Equipment in the gang’s Stash can be sold or transferred to a new model in the 
Post-cycle Sequence.
5. REPORT RESULTS
Finally, the players should report the outcome of the battle to the Arbitrator of 
their campaign, who will update the campaign stats accordingly. The report 
should contain: which gangs fought, the outcome of the battle (i.e., who won), 
which Territory the victor gained control of (if any) and how many enemies each 
gang took Out of Action.

THE POST-CYCLE SEQUENCE
At the end of a Campaign Cycle, all gangs take the chance for a brief cessation of hostilities in order to tend to their wounded, work their Territories and acquire new 
models/equipment.
The Post-cycle Sequence is comprised of the following steps:
1. Post-cycle Actions
2. Collect Income
3. Update Roster
1. POST-CYCLE ACTIONS
During a campaign, each model in the gang can perform one Post-cycle Action. 
Each of these actions is carried out one at a time, in an order of the controlling 
player’s choice. Models who are in Recovery or are Critically Injured/Damaged 
cannot perform Post-cycle Actions. Unless otherwise stated, the same action can 
be made more than once by different models. The actions below are always 
available, but others might also become available through special Territories, skills 
or other means.

The Fighter escorts a Critically Injured fellow gang member to the Doc. This action 
is resolved at the start of the Update Roster step. Select one other Fighter in the 
gang that suffered a Critical Injured during a battle and pay 30 credits from the 
gang’s Stash. If the gang does not have sufficient funds, or does not wish to pay the
full amount, no roll is made and the Fighter dies. Otherwise, roll a D6 on the table 
below:
D6 Result
1 Complications: The Fighter dies.
2-3 Stabilised:  Roll a D6 – this is the second dice of a D66 
roll; the first is automatically a 5. Apply the appropriate 
result from the Lasting Injury table.
4-6 Full Recovery: The Fighter goes into Recovery, but 
suffers no lasting effects.
“I’ll Use the Good Stuff”: If a player wishes, before rolling on the above table, they 
may pay for the Doc to use some of their more expensive supplies, which aids in 
getting a better result. For every extra 50 credits spent, add +1 to the roll.

The Fighter takes another Fighter to the Doc to be fitted with a bionic replacement.
This action is resolved at the start of the Update Roster step; select another Fighter

2. COLLECT INCOME

Gaining a New Skill: Whenever a model gains a new skill, it will usually either be from their Primary or Secondary Skill Sets. Selecting a new skill is covered in more 
detail later. If an Advancement roll for a model is 12, they may instead select a skill from any Skill Set, even those that are normally exclusive to other gangs, or 
inherent skills (note: a model may still not select a skill that is unavailable to their Type or Subtype).
B. VISIT THE TRADING POST
During this sub-step a gang may Recruit new models and purchase or sell 
equipment in an order as described below.
Recruit New Models: Gangs take this opportunity to recruit new models from their
gang lists.
Purchase Equipment: Gangs may spend their credits on equipment from their 
equipment lists for their gangs and purchase items from the Trading Post. Weapons
and wargear may always be purchased from a gang's equipment list, but can only 
be purchased from the Trading Post if at least one Fighter performed the Visit 
Trading Post Post-cycle Action.
Exclusive items (those with a TP of E) may only be purchased if they appear on a 
model's equipment list. Each Fighter that performs the visit Trading Post Post-cycle 
Action adds Trade Points to the Gang; 2 TP if the action is performed by a Leader, 1 
TP if it is performed by a Champion. In order to purchase items from the Trading 
Post, as well as paying the Credit Cost you must deduct the number of Trade Points 
for the equipment from the total generated; any unspent Trade Points are lost at 
the end of this sub step.
Sell Unwanted Items: Any items in the gang’s Stash can be sold. The item is 
deleted from the Stash, and the gang gains half its value in credits, rounded up 
(to a minimum of 5 credits).
C. REDISTRIBUTE EQUIPMENT
During this sub-step, models may discard any weapons or wargear they are 
equipped with and be given equipment from the gang’s Stash in the following 
manner:
• Models may discard any weapons or wargear they are equipped with. Any 
weapons and wargear discarded in this way are placed in the gang’s Stash and 
may be given to other models.
• Any weapons or wargear in the gang’s Stash may be given to models to equip.
• Models may only be given Exclusive weapons or wargear if it appears on their 
gang's equipment list.
• Models may be given more than three weapons; if they are, they must have 
multiple Model Cards, each representing a different 'equipment set.'
D. CLEAN HOUSE
Clear all In Recovery boxes and delete dead, destroyed and retired models.

GANG CREATION
When founding a gang, players have a maximum number of credits to spend on models, weapons, and wargear chosen from the entries within their gang list. In 
general, the budget is 1,000 credits and this budget may not be exceeded during Gang Creation. Any unspent credits will be added to the gang's Stash if the gang has 
been founded for Campaign play. However, if a gang has been founded for Skirmish play, any unspent credits are simply lost.
CAMPAIGN PLAY
When founding a gang for Campaign play, the budget available is 1,000 credits. Should they wish, the Arbitrator can increase or decrease this budget. 
MODEL NAMES & TYPES
Each model available to a gang is of a named Type; this is the name given to that models rank within the gang hierarchy of their house. In addition, each model will 
have a Type duly noted in their gang list entry. Model Types and Subtypes are explained in more detail elsewhere. Very often the rules will refer to models by their 
Type or Subtype. In such cases, the rule is universal to all such models, regardless of their name. At other times, the rules will refer to a model by their specific name; 
'Orlock Gunner,' for example. In these cases, the rule is specific only to models with that name. 
FOUNDING AN ENDURING GANG
Once a gang has completed a Campaign, it may be marked as Enduring in the Campaign Wrap-up. When founding a new gang for a Campaign, players may 
use an Enduring gang they have previously controlled and explore what happens to the next generation.
GANG LEADER
The first step is to hire your gang's new Leader. The gang's previous Leader has permanently retired, their story told, and someone new within the gang has 
taken over the reins. An Enduring gang may not hire a Leader from their gang list; instead a Fighter from the previous iteration of the gang must be selected 
to be the gang's new Leader – hire the Fighter for their Credit Cost with all Lasting Injuries, XP, weapons, wargear and skills carrying over. They then follow all
of the steps outlined in Death of a Leader.
TRIUMPH EFFECTS
If the gang earned any Triumphs in the last Campaign they took part in, they select one and gain a bonus for the next Campaign based on the selected 
Triumph.
Triumph Benefit
Dominator The gang generates and starts the campaign with an additional territory.
Slaughterer After the gang has been founded, the gang gains 9 additional XP that can be distributed between models recruited from an old
gang. Only 3 XP can be given to any single model.
Creditor The gang starts with an extra 100 credits which are added to their starting Stash and may be spent when founding the Gang.
Warmonger The gang starts the campaign with an additional 2 Reputation and can choose to be the attacker or defender in their first 
battle, regardless of who issued the challenge.
Powerbroker The gang can choose to start the campaign with one of the following Hangers-on for free (including any options): Ammo-Jack, 
Dome Runner, Hive Watcher, Rogue Doc or Slopper.
These Triumph benefits are only retained for the gang's next campaign.
REHIRE GANG MODELS
In addition to hiring new models, an Enduring gang may hire any models from the previous iteration of the gang at gang creation for their Credits Value with 
all Lasting Injuries, XP, weapons, wargear and skills carrying over.
GANG COMPOSITION
The first step is to choose and purchase the models that make up the gang. Each of 
the different gang lists details all of the models available to that gang. These 
models are purchased by paying the Credit Cost shown in their entry from the 
budget available. Most gangs must always follow the rules presented below. In the 
case of any gangs that follow different rules, these will be presented as part of that 
gang's gang list:
• There must be one model with the Leader Subtype.
• The total number of models with the Champion, Brute or Hanger-on Subtype 
must always be less than or equal to the number of models without any of those 
Subtypes.
Should it occur during the course of a campaign that the number of models with 
the Champion, Brute or Hanger-on Subtype exceeds the total number of models 
without those Subtypes, the controlling player must either:
• Retire a number of models with the Champion, Brute or Hanger-on Subtype 
during the Post-cycle Sequence in order to correct the imbalance.
Or:
• Recruit fresh models without the Champion, Brute or Hanger-on Subtype 
during the Post-cycle Sequence in order to correct the imbalance.
DESIGNER'S NOTE: PETS
Pets are treated as wargear and as such are not counted when 
working out if a gang has too many models with the Champion, 
Brute or hanger on Subtype. However, many players find it handy to 
include Pets on the Gang Roster so that they can keep track of their 
XP, Lasting Injuries, whether they are In Recovery and if they have 
performed a Post-cycle Action.
WEAPONS & WARGEAR
The next step is to choose and purchase the weapons on wargear each model will
be equipped with. Each models entry within their gang list includes a detailed list 
of the weapons and wargear that model may purchase.

=== PAGE 29 ===
All models can be equipped as follows:
• A Fighter can be equipped with a maximum of three weapons purchased from 
those listed in their entry.
• Grenades are counted as wargear and not weapons and so do not count towards 
this maximum.
• A Vehicle's entry will state if there is a maximum number of weapons it may be 
equipped with.
• Weapons marked with an asterisk (*) take up the space of two weapons.
• Any model may purchase wargear that is listed in the relevant equipment list.
• Each weapon may have at most one weapon accessory added to it.
EQUIPMENT SETS
Gang members often maintain an individual cache of weapons that allows them to equip themselves appropriately for each battle. As such, all models can 
have multiple Model Cards, each representing a different set of equipment:
• The controlling player can make an additional Model Card for a model at any time.
• An appropriate model should be available for each different equipment set a model has.
• There is no additional cost for having multiple equipment sets. If, for example, an Orlock Road Captain owns a two-handed hammer, they may include that 
weapon in as many or as few different equipment sets as the controlling player wishes, without additional cost. The weapon is only purchased and paid for 
once.
• Regardless of the number of equipment sets a model has, they still have a single entry on the Gang Roster. This entry should include the total cost of the 
individual weapons and items of wargear that the model owns. But remember, each individual weapon or item of wargear is only counted once.
• If the model suffers any Lasting Injuries/Damage or gains any Advancements, it should be recorded on all of their Model Cards.
• Only one of a model’s cards can be used for a battle.
• If a battle uses random models from the gang, all of the models Model Cards should be shuffled together and one should be drawn at random and added 
to the rest of the gang's Model Cards before any cards are drawn. This means that only one of this model's Model Cards can be drawn for the battle, and 
that the controlling player cannot choose which of their equipment sets they will be using.
• When distributing equipment from the gang’s Stash, it can be moved to any number of Model Cards for that model.
MODEL CARDS
Finally, a blank Model Card should be completed for each model when they are 
added to the gang; the characteristics of the model and any equipment they now 
have should be noted down in the appropriate sections of the model’s Model Card.
DEATH OF A LEADER
All gangs must include a single Fighter with the Leader Subtype. This Fighter is, 
naturally, the leader of the gang.
If a gang's Leader is killed, a new Leader must be nominated:
The new Leader is the Fighter with the highest Leadership characteristic, selected 
from among those Fighters that have, in order of priority:
1. The Champion Subtype, but not the Loner Subtype.
2. Both the Champion and Loner Subtype.
3. Neither the Beast, Brute, Pet or support Subtype.
Note that Vehicles and Hangers-on can never become a gang's Leader; if the only 
models left in a gang are Vehicles and Hangers-on, the gang is dissolved.
If two or more eligible Fighters have the same Leadership characteristic, use total 
XP as a tiebreaker; if there is still a tie, the controlling player can decide which 
Fighter will become the new gang Leader.
When a Fighter is promoted in this way, they gain the Leader Subtype, and from 
now on their role changes to that appropriate for their gang for the purposes of 
determining which equipment and Skill Sets they can access (for example, should 
an Orlock Road Sergeant be promoted to Leader, not only does their Subtype 
change from Champion to Leader, but their role becomes 'Orlock Road Captain'). 
They lose the Loner, Champion, Ganger and Prospect Subtypes if they have them,
though their existing characteristics and special rules do not change. Any 
weapons or wargear they are currently equipped with is retained even if it is not 
allowed by their new role, but they may no longer purchase additional weapons 
or wargear only granted to their original role.

THE GANG ROSTER
As well as filling in a Model Card for each model in their gang, players will need to complete a Gang Roster. This tracks additional information during campaign play, 
such as territories held, Gang Rating, Reputation, Wealth and so on, as well as each model's Experience and Advancements. During Skirmish play, such information is 
of less importance, but a Gang Roster is still a useful tool.
COMPLETING A GANG ROSTER
The Gang Roster is made up of a number of elements, as shown opposite. Many of these elements are explained over the following pages; those that are not are dealt 
with in detail in the campaign rules. These elements include the gang's name and Type, its Gang Rating, Reputation and unspent credits, Wealth, the Territories it 
holds, a list of the Triumphs used in the campaign, the gang's progress towards them and its Stash, containing surplus equipment (both weapons and wargear).
The Gang Roster also has a line for each model in the gang, with space for their names, model Type, Credit Cost, Experience earned, the amount of XP needed for their
next Advancement, and whether they are currently In Recovery or have suffered any Lasting Injuries/Damage.
Note that, although the number of rows is limited, there is no upper limit on the number of models a gang can contain. Should a gang be lucky enough to have more 
models than can be fitted onto one Gang Roster, simply use a second Gang Roster for extra space.
GANG ATTRIBUTES
In campaign play, gangs have a number of attributes, which are tracked on their 
Gang Roster. These attributes, and their starting values, are explained below:
GANG RATING
The gang rating is an indicator of how powerful the gang is – the proficiency of its 
models, how well-equipped they are, and so on. The Gang Rating is the total cost 
of all the models in the gang. 
REPUTATION
Reputation is the measure of a gang's standing and power in their region of the 
underhive. It is separate from Gang Rating, which is a measure of the gang's size 
and resources; a small gang can build up quite an impressive Reputation. A gang's 
Reputation starts at 1, and cannot drop below 1.
WEALTH
In campaign play, gangs also have a wealth value – this is equal to the total cost of 
all the models in the gang, plus the value of any credits or equipment they have in 
their Stash. 
MODEL CAMPAIGN ATTRIBUTES
In campaign play, each model in a gang has a number of attributes that are 
tracked in their entry on the Gang Roster: 
EXPERIENCE (XP)
Models earn XP during battles and in the Post-cycle Sequence; this section lets 
you track how much XP they currently have and how much they need to gain 
their next Advancement. 
IN RECOVERY
When a model goes Out of Action during a battle fought as part of a campaign, 
there is a chance that they will suffer a Lasting Injury or Lasting Damage. Many of 
these will cause them to go into recovery and miss the rest of the cycle. When 
this is the case, their In Recovery box is ticked.
Similarly any Lasting Injuries/Damage that have a long-term effect on the model 
are also recorded on the Gang Roster.
USING THE CAMPAIGN
Necromunda campaigns are based around gangs fighting battles for the control of territory. Each battle in the campaign is fought for dominance in the underhive with 
the winner having a chance of claiming a Territory or seizing one from their opponent.
Territories are features of the underhive that are worth something to the gangs, be that a set of old ruins full of ancient scrap, a safe haven, or a tech bazaar selling all 
manner of STC technology. Each player starts with two Territories based on the campaign and the Gang they are running. These are a Settlement Territory which they 
cannot lose and can never be staked on the outcome of a battle, representing the gang's Hideout, and one generated randomly (see Territories).
Possession of a Territory grants a gang that holds it a benefit called a Boon. Boons vary from income, to equipment and even recruits for the gang that controls them.
The campaign ends after a fixed period of time, and at that point gangs are awarded Triumphs based on how many battles they have fought, how much territory they 
have, how wealthy they are, how good their reputation is, and how many enemies they have taken down.
THE ARBITRATOR
Each Necromunda campaign requires one of the players to take on the role of an 
Arbitrator. It is the Arbitrator's job to keep track of Victories, offer guidance to the 
players and control any random events or elements. In a Necromunda campaign, 
the Arbitrators responsibilities include:
• Organizing the players and making a list of all the gangs involved in the campaign.
• Creating the Territory table and Scenario tables.
• Keeping the players informed as to the progression of the campaign and keeping 
track of the control of Territories.
• Deciding when each phase of the campaign begins and ends, as well as when the 
overall campaign starts and finishes.
THE GANGS
Each player in a Necromunda campaign needs to have a gang. This can be any 
gang currently available in any of the Necromunda supplements, or those 
published in White Dwarf magazine or on the Warhammer Community website.
CONTROLLING TERRITORIES
Each Territory is a feature of key interest in the localized area of the underhive 
and wastes within which the gangs are fighting. A gang gains a Territory by 
winning a battle and holds onto it by winning future battles fought over its 
possession.

PRE-BATTLE, POST-BATTLE and POST-CYCLE 
SEQUENCE
A Necromunda campaign uses all of the standard rules for the Pre-battle, Post-
battle and Post-cycle Sequences presented earlier in this book, including the 
gaining of XP and Advancements.
WINNING
Rather than a single winner, there are many Triumphs that a gang can achieve in a 
campaign. At the end of the last campaign cycle, the campaign is over. The 
Arbitrator then compares each gang's total enemies taken Out of Action, final 
wealth, final reputation, and the number of Territories held by each gang, and then
Awards Triumphs to players.
The Gang Roster has a section where players can record the Triumphs and keep 
track of how they are progressing towards each one.
SETTING UP THE CAMPAIGN
Setting up a campaign is a task that falls to the Arbitrator. To set up the campaign, 
the Arbitrator must make sure that all the players have founded their gang and 
have been given their starting Territories. This is best done in a single evening or 
session, where everyone playing in the campaign meets and goes through all the 
steps of setting up the campaign together, and generally gets excited about the fun 
ahead.
SET START AND END DATES
A Necromunda campaign is typically divided into six campaign Cycles, separated by 
a single cycle of Downtime after the first three Cycles, meaning the campaign will 
last a total of seven Cycles. For ease, we have set each Cycle as a week, as this is 
usually how often gaming groups or groups of friends get together to play; 
however, if the players choose, then a Cycle can represent a longer or shorter 
period of real time. The first step in setting up the campaign is for the Arbitrator to 
determine the start date, the end date and when the downtime Cycle will fall. They
then share this information with the players.

the territory table.

ISSUING & ACCEPTING CHALLENGES
During a campaign, battles are fought on a challenge basis:
• During each cycle, each player may issue one challenge to another gang to do 
battle over control of a territory nominated by the Challenger, 
- In the first Campaign Cycle, challenges are made in a random order. 
- In later Cycles, they are made in ascending order of Gang Rating, 
starting with the gang with the lowest Gang Rating. If two gangs have the same 
Gang Rating , the winner of a roll off makes their challenge first. 
- During the occupation phase, the Challenger nominates one 
uncontrolled Territory to be the Stake of the battle. This is then removed from 
the pool by the Arbitrator until the battle is concluded. If there are no 
uncontrolled Territories, the challenger instead nominates one Territory 
controlled by the gang they are challenging to be the Stake of the battle. 
- During the Takeover phase, the challenger nominates one Territory 
controlled by the gang they are challenging to be the Stake of the battle. 
• When a gang is challenged, they may accept or decline. If the challenge is 
accepted, a battle is fought. If the challenge is declined, the Territory is 
automatically claimed by the Challenger. 
• If a gang receives a challenge after they have issued a challenge, or receives two
or more challenges within a single Campaign Cycle, they may decline such 
challenges without losing control of any territories. 
• If a player has played all outstanding battles (including their issued challenge 
and any challenges accepted) and there is still time left in the Campaign Cycle, 
they may issue another challenge. 
The gang that wins the battle takes control of the Territory (or retains control of 
the Territory, as the case may be). If the battle results in a draw: 
• During the occupation phase, neither gang takes control of the Territory if the 
Territory is unclaimed, and it goes back into the pool. 
• During the Takeover phase, the gang that controlled the Territory before the 
battle retains control of the Territory.

SCENARIO SELECTION
When a challenge is issued, roll on the tables to generate the Scenario (see 
Scenario Generation Tables). For all Scenarios, the player that issued the challenge 
is the attacker.
RUNNING THE CAMPAIGN
A Necromunda campaign is broken down into two main parts divided by a period 
of Downtime, allowing gangs to lick their wounds and consolidate their strength 
before the final, bloody confrontation is played out. As a guideline, each part runs 
for three Cycles in which battles are played and victories are tallied. At the end of 
each Cycle (as determined by the Arbitrator), the effects of the players’ battles are 
applied to the campaign and gangs go through the Post-cycle Sequence. 
A Necromunda campaign is divided into three phases, all of which run for a fixed 
length of real time: 
1. Occupation Phase (Three Cycles)
2. Downtime (One Cycle)
3. Takeover Phase (Three Cycles)
ARBITRATOR’S TOOLKIT: Varying Campaign Length
Presented here are the rules for running a standard length 
Necromunda campaign. If the Arbitrator wishes, they may extend or 
shorten the length of any phase or even add additional phases when 
setting up their campaign. Just make sure to let all the players know!
Each phase is broken down as follows:
1. OCCUPATION PHASE (3 CYCLES)
The first part of the campaign follows the gangs as they try to increase the 
number of Territories they control and grow their influence. During this phase, 
the following rules apply:
• The winner of a battle adds 2D3x10 credits to their Stash and increases their 
Reputation by 1.
• The loser of a battle adds D3x10 credits to their Stash. 
• If the battle was a draw, both gangs add D3x10 credits to their Stash.
• If a gang completes its side job, they add D3x10 credits to their Stash.
• Only unclaimed Territories may be claimed as rewards for gang battles unless 
there are no unclaimed territories (see Issuing and Accepting Challenges).
2. DOWNTIME (1 CYCLE)
During Downtime, gangs have a chance to recover and regroup, taking the 
opportunity to re-equip. After their last bottle of the occupation phase, players 
get the chance to add some fresh models to their gang before the final push to 
the end of the campaign. 
All gangs gain 250 credits to spend on new equipment from their gang equipment
list and/or to recruit new models during this Cycle’s Post-cycle Sequence. These 
credits must be spent now and cannot be added to the gang's Stash. Gangs may 
supplement these credits with extra credits from their Stash. 
3. TAKEOVER PHASE (3 CYCLES)
The second part of the campaign sees gangs defend their Territories against their 
rivals, as well as capture those controlled by other gangs. During this phase the 
following rules apply:
• The winner of a battle adds 2D6x10 credits to their Stash and increases their 
reputation by 2.
• The loser of a battle adds D6x10 credits to their Stash.
• If the battle was a draw, both gangs add D6x10 credits to their Stash.
• If a gang completes its side job, they add D6x10 credits to their Stash.
• If a gang loses control of a Territory, they reduce their reputation by 1.
• Only controlled Territories may be fought over and claimed as a Rewards (see 
Issuing and Accepting Challenges).

CAMPAIGN WRAP-UP
Once a campaign is finished, all players go through the campaign Wrap-up together. This is where players review their successes and failures in the campaign, Triumphs
are awarded and models/gangs prepared for future campaigns. The campaign Wrap-up is split into three phases: 
1. Report Results
2. Award Triumphs
3. Gang Dissolution
1. REPORT RESULTS
Each player makes sure they report how their gang did against each Triumph to the Arbitrator so they can award Triumphs. 
2. AWARD TRIUMPHS
Victory in the Necromunda campaign can be measured in many ways, although simply surviving the fierce inter-gang warfare is an achievement in itself! During this 
step, the Arbitrator awards Triumphs to the players depending on how their gangs have performed during the campaign. If multiple gangs are tied for a Triumph, it is 
awarded to the gang with the highest wealth. If they are still tied, all tied gangs are awarded the Triumph. 
Triumph Criteria
Dominator Most Territories at the end of the campaign.
Slaughterer Most enemy models taken Out of Action across the whole campaign.
Creditor Highest wealth at the end of the campaign.
Warmonger Most battles fought across the whole campaign.
Powerbroker Highest reputation at the end of the campaign.
3. GANG DISSOLUTION
Nothing on Necromunda lasts forever, and gangs are no exception. At the end of a campaign, gangs break apart as some Fighters retire, others go on to create their 
own gangs, and the Leadership of the gang passes on to new hands. However, even though they have changed, many gangs live on – few can remember a time where 
the Sump City Sirens did not claim dominion (though not always legitimately) over Sump City, though the members of the gang have changed countless times. 
If players wish, they may mark a gang as enduring and select one of the Triumphs the gang earned to grant them a bonus in the next campaign they take part in.
TERRITORIES
This section gives an overview of what each Territory represents and details the bonuses the Territory grants to the gang that holds it.
All gangs possess a Settlement Territory they cannot lose and which can never be Staked on the outcome of a battle. This represents the gang's Hideout.
TERRITORY BOONS
Each Territory grants the gang that controls it a benefit called a Boon. This Boon 
might be income, and additional gang member, and increase in Reputation, a piece 
of useful equipment or a Special Rule.
A gang receives the benefits of a Territory's Boon so long as it holds that Territory. 
Once the Territory is lost to another gang, the Boon is lost.
INCOME
If a Territory provides income for the gang that controls it, the gang adds the 
amount of credits shown to their Stash when generating income during the Post-
cycle Sequence.
RECRUIT
Some Territories grant gangs new models as recruits to the gang. These recruits 
incur no cost but do add to the Gang Rating and Wealth value. Others let the gang 
recruit models at a discount. These models still add their full cost to the Gang 
Rating and Wealth value. 
Regardless of whether the model is free or discounted, any equipment that is not 
part of the model’s basic cost must be purchased at the normal cost, or supplied 
from the gang’s Stash. 
Any model recruited as a Boon will remain with the gang, even if the gang later 
loses the Territory. 
EQUIPMENT
The gang adds the listed equipment type to its Stash. It is gained during the 
Collect Income step of the Post-cycle Sequence and is added to the gang's Stash. 
It may then be distributed to models as normal. Equipment gained in this manner
can be sold in later Post-cycle Sequences and is not lost if the Territory that 
granted it is not lost. 
REPUTATION
The gang's Reputation is increased by the listed value as a direct result of 
ownership of that Territory. If they lose control of the Territory, the bonus is lost. 
SPECIAL
The Boon grants a unique benefit to the gang that holds the Territory. The benefit
follows the rules listed in the relevant entry for the Territory. If the Territory is 
ever lost, then so is the benefit. 
GAINING & CONTROLLING TERRITORIES
Each gang starts with a Settlement Territory and one other that is randomly 
generated. The only other way to gain Territory is to win a battle where a 
Territory is the Stake.

BATTLEFIELD SET-UP
The default method for setting up the battlefield is as follows:
UNDERHIVE
The battlefields of the underhive vary vastly, from complexes of cramped tunnels, raucous marketplaces, rogue factoria and foundries to gang strongholds and even 
bustling settlements. The thing they all have in common is that they are fully enclosed, protected from the unpredictable and harsh weather of the wastes. 
For underhive battles, between a 3'x3' and a 4'x4' battlefield is ideal, although much larger or smaller battlefields are also possible. Starting with the defender, players 
alternate placing terrain features upon the battlefield. Once one player declines to place any terrain, then the other player may place one more piece of terrain. 
Alternatively, if using Zone Mortalis tiles, players alternate placing the tiles on the battlefield.
Then, starting with the defender again, players alternate placing obstacles and other small terrain features. Once one player passes, the other player may place one 
more obstacle. 
TUNNEL WARFARE
When creating an underhive battlefield, the default is that the ceiling is high up above the ground and so walls can be climbed up onto and stairs and ladders used. 
However, if both players agree, the claustrophobic confines of tunnel battles can be recreated by stating that the ceiling is at the height of one wall section, meaning 
walls cannot be climbed over, and stairs and ladders cannot be used. 
CREW SELECTION
Each player chooses a starting crew for the Scenario, using one of the following 
methods as determined by the crew table. 
RANDOM SELECTION (X)
When the crew table instructs players to use Random Selection (X) to choose their 
starting crew, the following method is used: 
• The player makes a deck of their Model Cards. The deck may not include any 
models unable to take part in the battle. 
• The deck may only contain one randomly selected Model Card for any models 
who have multiple Model Cards. 
• This deck is then shuffled and a number of Model Cards equal to the number 
shown in brackets is dealt out. This is the player's starting crew. 
CUSTOM SELECTION (X)
When the Crew table instructs players to use Custom Selection (X) to choose their 
starting crew, the following method is used:
• The player chooses their crew, selecting the Model Cards of the models they wish
to use, equal to the numbers shown in brackets. 
• If any models have multiple Model Cards, the player chooses which equipment 
set the model will use for the battle. 
• Models that are unable to take part in the battle may not be selected. 
HYBRID SELECTION (X+Y)
When the crew table instructs players to use Hybrid Selection (X+Y) to choose their
starting crew, the following method is used:
• The player chooses a number of models equal to the first number (X) to be part 
of their starting crew and sets their Model Cards to one side.
- If any models have multiple Model Cards, the player chooses which 
equipment load-out the model will use for the battle.
- Models that are unable to take part in the battle may not be selected.
• The player then makes a deck of any remaining Model Cards.
- If any models have multiple Model Cards, randomly determine which 
Model Card is added to the deck.
- Models that are unable to take part in the battle may not be included 
in the deck.
• The deck is then shuffled and a number of Model Cards equal to the second 
number (Y) shown in brackets are dealt out and added to their starting crew.
REINFORCEMENTS (X)
If the crew table says that a gang has Reinforcements (X) available, then all 
models who are not part of the starting crew and able to take part in the battle 
are shuffled together and then a number of Model Cards equal to X are dealt out 
to form the Reinforcements deck. If a model has multiple Model Cards, randomly 
determine which one is added to the Reinforcements deck. 
• Reinforcements always arrive in the Reinforcements step of the End phase. The 
crew table will state the round(s) on which Reinforcements arrive and how many.
• Starting with the player who won Priority this round, players take turns 
randomly drawing Reinforcements from their Reinforcements deck. They 
continue alternating drawing cards until each player has either deployed the 
number of Reinforcements that are due to arrive this round, or they have run out 
of Model Cards in their Reinforcements deck. For each model drawn, a D6 is 
rolled: 
- On a 3+, the controlling player deploys them on any battlefield edge 
at least 9" away from all enemy models. 
- On a 1-2, the controlling player deploys them on any battlefield edge 
at least 12" away from all enemy models. 
DEPLOYMENT
The Deployment table section will detail where the deployment zones are for the 
battle. When deploying models, starting with the defender, players alternate 
placing a model in their deployment zone. If one player finishes deploying their 
starting crew while the other players still has models to deploy, then all remaining
models are deployed. 
VISIBILITY (X")
Battles on Necromunda are not always fought in bright light, either because of 
the smog of industry filling the underhive, lumens failing due to a lack of 
maintenance, or sporadic power outages. 
These conditions are represented by the following special rules: 
• Models cannot draw a Line of Sight to a target more than X" away unless the 
target is using a photo-lumen. Models using photo-lumens, photo-goggles or 
infra-sights extend this range by 9" (note: these and similar effects do not stack).

D6 Objective
1 King of the Hive: Place a single objective in the centre of the battlefield. During the Check For Victory step of the End phase, it is worth 1 VP. The winner

2 Turf War: From round three onward, if only one player has models who are not Seriously Injured/Damaged within 9" of the centre of the battlefield

two objectives, they score one VP. The winner is the first player to reach three VP. If only one player has at least one model on the battlefield, they are 
victorious. If neither player has any models on the battlefield, the battle is a draw. 
4 Object Lesson: During the Check For Victory step of the End phase, both gangs earn VP for any enemy models that went Out of Action that round. One 
VP is earned for each enemy model that went Out of Action; an additional 1 VP is earned if the model had the Leader, Champion or Brute Subtype, and 
an additional 1 VP if they are taken Out of Action as a part of a Fight, Coup de Grâce or Sabotage action. The winner is the first player to reach 10 VP. If 
only one player has at least one model on the battlefield, they are victorious. If both players have at least 10 VP, or neither player has any models on the
battlefield, the battle is a draw. 
5 Flank ‘em: The attacker picks a battlefield edge and places an objective in the centre of it, 6" onto the battlefield. The defender then places an objective 
in the same position on the opposite battlefield edge. During the Check For Victory step of the End phase, if a gang controls both objectives they earn 1

models on the battlefield, the battle is a draw.
6 Burn Them Out: Starting with the defender, each player takes turns placing an objective in their opponents deployment zone until they have each 
placed three objectives. Each objective must be deployed at least 6" from all other objectives, if possible. During the Check For Victory step of the End 
phase, if a gang controls an objective in their opponent's deployment zone they score 1 VP and remove the objective. The winner is the first player to 
reach 2 VP. If only one player has at least one model on the battlefield, they are victorious. If both players have at least 2 VP or neither player has any 
models on the battlefield, the battle is a draw.

AGILITY
1. CATFALL (FIGHTER OR WALKER ONLY)
When this model falls or jumps down from a ledge, they count the vertical distance
as being one level less than normal. In addition, if they are not Seriously 
Injured/Damage or taken Out of Action by a fall, make an Agility test for them – if 
it is passed, they do not become Suppressed.
2. CLAMBER (FIGHTER ONLY)
When this Fighter climbs, the distance they move is not halved. In other words, 
they always count as climbing up or down a ladder.
3. DODGE (FIGHTER OR VEHICLE)
If this model suffers a Wound from a shooting or close combat attack (including as 
a result of the Unstable trait), roll a D6. On a 6, the attack is dodged and has no 
further effect; otherwise, continue to make a save or resolve the wound as normal.
If the model dodges a weapon that uses a Blast marker or flame Template, a roll of 
6 does not automatically cancel the attack – instead, it allows the model to move 
up to 2" before seeing if they are Hit. They cannot move within 1" of an enemy 
model.
4. MIGHTY LEAP (FIGHTER OR WALKER ONLY)
When measuring the distance of a gap a model wishes to leap across, ignore the 
first 2" of the distance. This means that a model with this skill may leap over gaps 
of 4" or less without making an Agility test. All other rules for leaping over gaps will
apply.
5. SPRING UP (FIGHTER ONLY)
If this Fighter is Suppressed when they are activated, make an Agility test for them.
If the test is passed, the Fighter is no longer Suppressed and may perform two 
actions as normal.
6. SPRINT (FIGHTER OR WALKER ONLY)
While Active, this model may perform the Sprint action:
Sprint (Double): The model may move a distance equal to their Movement 
characteristic plus two times their Initiative characteristic in inches. Any effect that 
replaces a model's Initiative characteristic with a different number for the purpose

WEAPON TRAITS
The following are the most common traits for use in battles of Necromunda.
ADDITIONAL ATTACKS (X)
The weapon can be used to make a number of attacks during close combat equal 
to X in addition to all normal attacks. This only applies during the model's 
activation and only if the weapon is not designated as either the models Primary or
Secondary weapon.
AMMO (X)
After a weapon with this trait has fired, roll a D6. If the result is X or more, there is 
ammo left in the weapon. Otherwise, it gains the Out of Ammo Condition 
automatically, even if no Firepower dice are rolled. If an Out of Ammo result is also
rolled for it on the Firepower dice in addition to Rolling under X, then the weapon 
jams and cannot be used again for the duration of the battle.
ARC (X)
A weapon with this trait has limited fields of fire, as denoted by X. A model can 
only target models within the field of fire with a weapon with this trait. 
ASSAULT
After the bearer has performed a Dash action, they may perform a Shoot action 
with this weapon as a Free action. 
AUXILIARY
A weapon with this trait can only be attached to another weapon and not taken on 
its own. This weapon does not use one of the model’s three weapon slots. 
BACKSTAB
This weapon gains +1 Strength if the target of the attack is Engaged with more than
one model.
BLAST (3"/5")
When firing a weapon with the Blast (3"/5") trait, place either the 3" or 5" Blast 
marker centred on the model that is being targeted. If the Hit roll succeeds, the 
template stays where it is. If it misses scatter it by moving it D6 " in a random 
direction determined by the Scatter dice (if the Hit symbol is rolled, follow the 
direction of the small arrow above the hit), stopping if it hits impassable terrain or 
would go off the battlefield. If a Hit symbol and a 1 are rolled on the Scatter dice 
then the shot misfired: centre the blast marker on the firing model. Any model 
with their base at least partially under the Blast marker suffers one automatic Hit. 
If a weapon has both the Blast (3"/5") and the Rapid Fire (X) traits, resolve the first 
Blast marker as usual. Any additional Hits that would be caused due to the number 
of bullet holes across all the firepower dice are resolved as Blast markers that 
scatter from the final location of the first Blast marker. 
BLAZE (X)
If the Wound roll for an attack with a weapon with this trait is at least X, then the 
target suffers an additional Hit with the same profile as the first, even if it fails to 
Wound. This additional Hit must roll to Wound separately and cannot cause further
additional Hits. A weapon may never benefit from more than one instance of this 
trait; if a weapon has this trait multiple times, only the one with the lowest value of
X is used. 
BREACHING (X+)
If an attack from a weapon with this trait successfully Wounds its target and the 
Wound roll is at least X, then Armour saves cannot be made against the attack. 
COMBI
When a weapon with this trait is fired as part of a Shoot or Aimed Shot action, the 
model’s controller can choose which profile to resolve the attack with before rolling
the dice. If they wish, they can attack with both profiles targeting the same model, 
applying a -1 modifier to the Hit roll for each profile. These attacks are resolved 
simultaneously. 
CONCUSSIVE (X+)
If an attack from a weapon with this trait successfully Wounds its target and the 
Wound roll is at least X, then the target of the attack reduces their Initiative 
characteristic by 1 until the end of their next activation. 
CURSED
A model Hit by a weapon with the Cursed trait must pass a Willpower check or 
become subject to the Insanity Condition.
 
If the Hit roll for a weapon with the cursed trait is a natural 1 then the bearer 
must pass a Willpower check or become subject to the Insanity condition. 
DAMAGE (X)
If a model is Wounded by an attack with a weapon with this trait, they lose X 
Wounds rather than the usual one. Note that if this results in Injury dice being 
rolled, the number rolled is equal to the Lethality characteristic of the weapon, 
regardless of the number of Wounds lost. 
DRAG
If a model is Hit by a weapon with the Drag trait but not taken Out of Action, the 
attacker can attempt to drag the target closer after the attack has been resolved. 
If they do, roll a D6. If the score is equal to or higher than the target's Strength 
characteristic, the target is dragged D3 " directly towards the attacker, stopping if 
they hit any terrain. If they move into another model (other than the attacker), 
both models are moved the remaining distance directly towards the attacker. This
movement can cause models to fall. Once this movement has been resolved, any 
models that are now within 1" of an enemy model are moved the shortest 
distance possible to be Engaged with that model. 
FLASH
If a model is Hit by a weapon with the Flash trait, no Wound roll is made. Instead,
the target model must make an Initiative check. If the check is failed, they 
become subject to the Blind condition. A Blind model loses their Ready marker; if
they do not have a Ready marker, they do not gain a Ready marker at the start of 
the following round. Until the next time the model is activated, any close combat 
attacks they make will only Hit on rolls of a natural 6.
GAS
Armour saves cannot be taken against attacks made by a weapon with this trait. 
Models that are equipped with a respirator gain a 5+ Invulnerable save against 
Wounds caused by a weapon with this trait. 
GRAVITON PULSE
Instead of rolling to Wound with this weapon, any model caught in the Blast area 
must instead make a Strength check. If the check is failed, they are Wounded 
with no Armour saves allowed. After the weapon has been fired, leave the Blast 
marker in place. For the remainder of the round, any model moving through this 
area will treat it as difficult terrain. At the start of each End phase, roll a D6 for 
each Blast marker placed in this manner. On a result of a 5+, the Blast marker 
remains in place for another round, otherwise it is removed from the battlefield. 
HEAVY
A ranged weapon with this trait can only be fired using the Braced Shot action. 
A close combat weapon with this trait may not be selected as a Secondary 
weapon. Additionally, a Secondary weapon may not be used alongside this 
weapon.

INDEPENDENT
A weapon with this trait cannot be fired by the bearer of the weapon. Instead, it is 
fired in addition to other weapons whenever the bearer performs a ranged attack, 
and may target a different model to the bearer. When a weapon with this trait is 
fired, all hits are resolved with a BS of 4+ (note: this cannot be modified by the 
bearer's skills, equipment or special rules and does not benefit from the Aimed 
Shot action).
KNOCKBACK (X+)
If a weapon with this trait hits a Fighter and the Hit roll is at least X, then the 
Fighter hit is moved 1" directly away from the attacker. If this would cause them to 
fall off a ledge, they test for falling. This can also cause models to no longer be 
Engaged and so be unable to attack back during later Initiative steps. 
If a weapon has both this trait and the blast (3"/5") trait, then after the final 
position of the Blast marker has been determined, roll a D6 for each Fighter Hit by 
the Blast marker. If the result of the roll is at least X then the Fighter is moved 1" 
directly away from the centre of the Blast marker. If the Fighter cannot move back 
due to either impassable terrain, the battlefield edge or the move putting them 
within 1" of an enemy Fighter, they are not moved. 
LANCE
While equipped by a Fighter that has the Mounted Subtype, any weapon with this 
trait increases its Strength characteristic by 1 for any attacks made as part of a 
Charge action. 
LANCE-BOMB
The first successful Hit scored by a weapon with this trait each battle is resolved 
using the Primed profile; all subsequent hits are resolved using the Spent profile. 
LIGHT
A weapon with this trait may be used as either a Primary or Secondary weapon in 
close combat, though only one attack may be made with this weapon regardless of 
the models Attacks characteristics or any skills or weapon traits. 
If a weapon has both the Template and the Light trait, then if it is used in close 
combat one opposing enemy model suffers one automatic Hit – do not place the 
template. 
LIMITED
If a weapon with the Limited trait runs out of ammo then the weapon cannot be 
reloaded and may not be used again for the remainder of the battle. 
MELEE
A weapon with this trait is used to make attacks while Engaged. 
PAIRED (X)
A weapon with this trait can never be selected as a Secondary weapon. 
Additionally, a Secondary weapon may not be used alongside this weapon. When 
this weapon is selected as a Primary weapon, the bearer increases their Attacks 
characteristic by X. 
PARRY
If a model uses a weapon with this trait as either their Primary or Secondary 
weapon when resolving a combat, they increase their Save characteristic by 1 
(note: using more than one weapon with this trait does not confer any additional 
benefit).
POWER PACK
A weapon with this trait does not count towards the maximum number of weapons
a Fighter can carry. However, a Fighter cannot have more than two weapons with 
this trait. 
RAD-PHAGE
When a Fighter suffers an unsaved Wound from a weapon with this trait, they 
gain the Rad Poisoned Condition. While a Fighter is subject to the Rad Poisoned 
Condition they reduce their Toughness characteristic by 1, to a minimum of 1.
RAM
A weapon with this trait can only be used during an activation when the bearer 
has performed the Charge action. 
RAPID FIRE (X)
A weapon with this trait follows the rules for Rapid Fire (see Shooting).
RECKLESS
Weapons with the Reckless trait are indiscriminate in what they target. When 
resolving an attack with a weapon with the Reckless trait, the following rules 
apply: 
Before making a shooting attack with a Reckless weapon, randomly determine 
the target of the attack from all models (friend or foe) within this model's Line of 
Sight, 6" of the intended target and range of the weapon.
Attacks made with a Reckless close combat weapon are randomly distributed 
between any models (friend or foe) that are Engaged with this model. 
RELIABLE
A weapon with this trait ignores the first Out of Ammo symbol rolled each round 
on the Firepower dice. 
RENDING (X+)
If the natural Wound roll for a weapon with this trait is at least X then increase 
the AP characteristic of the weapon for this attack by 1 (i.e., AP -1 would become 
AP -2).
SCARCE (X+)
If a weapon has the Scarce (X+) trait then when the Reload action is performed, 
instead of the weapon automatically having more ammo the player must roll a 
D6. If the result is at least X then the weapon is successfully reloaded, otherwise 
the weapon is still Out of Ammo, though further attempts can be made in later 
activations. 
SHIELD
If a model is equipped with at least one weapon with this trait, they increase 
their Save characteristic by 1 against shooting attacks (note: a model gains no 
further benefit from being equipped with multiple weapons with this trait).
SHOCK (X+)
If an attack from a weapon with this trait Hits and the Hit roll is at least X then it 
automatically Wounds and is considered to have rolled a natural 6 on the Wound 
roll for the purposes of any other traits. 
SHRED (X+)
If a weapon with this trait Wounds a model and the Wound roll is at least X then 
increase the Lethality characteristic of the weapon for that attack by 1.
SINGLE SHOT
A weapon with this trait can only be fired once per battle, at which point it 
automatically goes Out of Ammo and cannot be reloaded by any means.

SMOKE
A weapon with this trait does not have to target an enemy model; it may instead 
target a point on the battlefield. After the final position of the Blast marker has 
been determined, leave the Blast marker in place on the battlefield – a column of 
smoke of infinite height rises up from the Blast marker which blocks Line of Sight. If
the column hits a terrain feature, it stops at the edge of the terrain feature.
During the Reinforcement step of the End phase, roll a D6 for each Blast marker 
placed by this trait; on a result of a 5+, the Blast marker remains in place, 
otherwise it is removed. 
A model Hit by a smoke grenade is never Wounded by the attack and they are not 
Suppressed. 
TEMPLATE
When a weapon with the Template trait is fired, place the teardrop shaped 
template so that the narrow end is in base contact with the firing model and the 
targeted model is under the template. Any model either partially under the 
template or with any part of their base under the template is automatically Hit, 
there is no need to roll to Hit.
If a weapon has both the Template and the Light trait then if it is used in combat 
one opposing enemy model suffers one automatic Hit; do not place the Template. 
If a weapon has both the Template and the Rapid Fire (X) traits then the model that
is closest to the firing model is Hit a number of times, as determined by the 
Firepower dice; all other models are Hit just once. 
TOXIN (X+)
When making a Wound roll for a weapon with this trait against a Fighter, instead 
of comparing the weapons Strength characteristic to the targets Toughness 
characteristic, the target is Wounded on a result of X+.
When making a Wound rule for a weapon with this trait against a Vehicle, instead
of comparing the weapon’s Strength characteristic to the target's Toughness 
characteristic, the target is only Wounded on a result of a natural 6.
TWIN-LINKED
When making a ranged attack with a weapon with this trait, the Firepower dice 
may be re-rolled (note: all the Firepower must be re-rolled).
UNSTABLE
If the Hit roll for a weapon with the Unstable trait is a natural 1, then the model 
using the weapon suffers an automatic Hit using this weapons profile and no 
other models are Hit by the weapon. If the weapon does not need to roll to Hit 
(for example, it has the Template trait) then roll a D6 before resolving the attack 
as described previously.
UNWIELDY
When a model attacks with a weapon with this trait in close combat, their 
Initiative is set to 1 before applying any modifiers. 
WEB
Armour saves cannot be taken against a weapon with this trait, though 
Invulnerable saves may still be taken. If a Fighter is Wounded by a weapon with 
the Web trait, then instead of losing a Wound, they gain the Webbed condition.
